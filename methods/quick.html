⚙️ CFD Guidance

✅ When to Use QUICK:

  • High-quality structured grids - Low skewness, good aspect ratios
  • Research applications - When accuracy is critical
  • Complex flow phenomena - Swirl, recirculation, mixing
  • Moderate Reynolds numbers - Re 10³-10⁶ range
  • Advanced CFD studies - Beyond engineering approximations
  • Validation cases - Comparing with experimental data

❌ When to Avoid QUICK:

  • Poor mesh quality - High skewness or irregular grids
  • Convergence issues - When stability is more important
  • Very high Reynolds numbers - May require limiters
  • Initial simulations - Overkill for preliminary studies
  • Unstructured meshes - Not optimal for irregular grids
  • Simple engineering analysis - Second order sufficient

✅ Advantages

  • Third-order spatial accuracy
  • Low numerical diffusion
  • Good for complex flow physics
  • Captures gradients well
  • Widely available in CFD codes

⚠️ Limitations

  • Requires high-quality meshes
  • Can be unstable on poor grids
  • Higher computational cost
  • May need slope limiters
  • More complex implementation

🔢 Mathematical Formulation

QUICK Interpolation Formula
$$\phi_f = \frac{6}{8}\phi_C + \frac{3}{8}\phi_F - \frac{1}{8}\phi_{CC}$$

Quadratic upstream interpolation using three points

Quadratic Polynomial
$$\phi(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$$

Fitted through three upstream points

Convection Boundedness Criterion
$$0 \leq \frac{\phi_f - \phi_C}{\phi_F - \phi_C} \leq 1$$

Boundedness check for physical solutions

Truncation Error
$$\text{Error} = O(\Delta x^3) + O(\Delta t)$$

Third-order accurate in space

🧠 Engineering Analogy

📈 Advanced Weather Forecasting

QUICK is like sophisticated weather prediction using trend analysis:

  • Three data points: "Use today, yesterday, and day before" - captures acceleration/deceleration
  • Quadratic fitting: Like fitting a curve through recent weather data
  • Better prediction: Can predict if trends are speeding up or slowing down
  • More sensitive: Like advanced forecasting - better accuracy but needs good data

The advancement: Like upgrading from linear trend to curved trend analysis - captures more physics but requires higher quality input data.

PIMPLE

Merged PISO-SIMPLE Algorithm

🎯 Practical Applications

⏰ Large Time Steps
Transient flows with relaxed temporal resolution requirements
🏭 Industrial Transients
Startup procedures, batch processes, thermal cycling
🌊 Complex Multiphase
VOF simulations, free surface flows, sloshing
🔄 Iterative Convergence
Balance between accuracy and computational efficiency

⚙️ CFD Guidance

✅ When to Use PIMPLE:

  • Large time step transients - CFL > 1 acceptable
  • Multiphase flows - VOF, Eulerian-Eulerian methods
  • Complex geometries - Moving meshes, deforming domains
  • Industrial simulations - Balance accuracy vs computational cost
  • OpenFOAM preference - Well-integrated implementation
  • Moderate accuracy needs - Engineering-level precision sufficient

❌ When to Avoid PIMPLE:

  • High-frequency phenomena - Small time scales requiring PISO
  • Research-level accuracy - DNS, high-fidelity LES
  • Steady-state problems - SIMPLE family more efficient
  • Very poor mesh quality - May cause convergence issues
  • Acoustic problems - Compressible solvers preferred
  • Simple single-phase flows - Overkill for basic problems

✅ Advantages

  • Allows larger time steps than PISO
  • Good for multiphase applications
  • Combines PISO accuracy with SIMPLE stability
  • Flexible convergence control
  • Handles complex physics well

⚠️ Limitations

  • More complex parameter tuning
  • Higher computational cost per time step
  • May require multiple outer iterations
  • Less temporal accuracy than pure PISO
  • Convergence can be sensitive to settings

🔢 Algorithm Steps

Outer Loop: SIMPLE-like Iterations
$$\text{for } i = 1 \text{ to } n_{outer} \text{ do}$$

Multiple outer iterations for convergence within each time step

Step 1: Momentum Predictor (with Under-Relaxation)
$$\alpha_u \frac{\partial \rho \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \mathbf{u}) = -\nabla p^* + \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\tau} + \rho \mathbf{g}$$

Solve momentum with under-relaxation (SIMPLE-like approach)

Step 2: PISO Pressure Corrections
$$\nabla \cdot \left( \frac{1}{a_p} \nabla p'_j \right) = \frac{1}{\Delta t} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}^{j-1}$$
$$\mathbf{u}^j = \mathbf{u}^{j-1} - \frac{\Delta t}{a_p} \nabla p'_j$$

Multiple PISO-style pressure corrections (j = 1, 2, ...)

Step 3: Convergence Check
$$\text{if } \|\mathbf{u}^{n+1,i} - \mathbf{u}^{n+1,i-1}\| < \epsilon \text{ then exit outer loop}$$

Check convergence within time step, continue outer iterations if needed

📊 Variables & Parameters

n_outer (-)
Number of outer iterations (typically 2-5)
n_correctors (-)
Number of PISO pressure corrections (1-3)
αᵤ (-)
Momentum under-relaxation (0.7-0.9)
Δt (s)
Time step - can be larger than pure PISO
CFL (-)
Courant number (CFL > 1 often acceptable)
ε (-)
Convergence tolerance for outer iterations

🧠 Engineering Analogy

🔄 Hybrid Project Management

PIMPLE is like combining agile sprints with traditional project management:

  • Outer Loop (SIMPLE-like): "Weekly project reviews" - check overall progress, adjust plans
  • Inner Loop (PISO-like): "Daily standups" - quick corrections and fine-tuning
  • Flexible timing: Like allowing longer sprints when needed, but still maintaining quality checks
  • Best of both worlds: Stability of traditional planning + responsiveness of agile methods

The hybrid approach: Like using different management styles for different project phases - conservative planning for stability, rapid iterations for accuracy.

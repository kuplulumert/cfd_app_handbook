SIMPLEC

SIMPLE-Consistent Algorithm with Improved Velocity Correction

1. Method Overview

  • Enhanced SIMPLE algorithm with improved velocity correction for better momentum-pressure coupling.
  • Consistent formulation addressing the omitted terms in standard SIMPLE velocity correction.
  • Industry adoption in commercial codes for improved steady-state convergence performance.
  • Developed by Van Doormaal and Raithby (1984) as a systematic improvement to SIMPLE.

2. Key Strengths

  • Faster convergence: Typically 20-30% improvement over standard SIMPLE for most applications.
  • Better coupling: Improved momentum-pressure consistency through enhanced velocity correction.
  • Higher under-relaxation: Can use momentum factors up to 0.8-0.9 compared to SIMPLE's 0.7.
  • Robust performance: Maintains SIMPLE's stability while providing enhanced convergence speed.
  • Easy implementation: Minimal code changes from SIMPLE, making adoption straightforward.

3. Limitations

  • Incremental improvement: Not dramatically faster than SIMPLE, still slower than coupled methods.
  • Transient limitations: Same issues as SIMPLE for highly unsteady flows.
  • Memory overhead: Slightly higher storage requirements for improved velocity correction.
  • Complex coupling: Still struggles with very strong pressure-velocity coupling scenarios.

4. Ideal Applications

All SIMPLE Applications: Direct replacement for SIMPLE with improved performance
Complex Geometries: Better handling of complex flow passages and geometries
Design Optimization: Parametric studies where faster convergence saves significant time
Industrial CFD: Manufacturing and process applications requiring reliable convergence
Heat Transfer: Conjugate heat transfer problems with moderate coupling
Educational Enhancement: Teaching improved pressure-velocity coupling concepts

5. When to Choose SIMPLEC Over Other Methods

  • SIMPLE replacement: When using SIMPLE but needing faster convergence without major changes.
  • Moderate complexity: Geometries more complex than simple channels but not requiring coupled solvers.
  • Computational efficiency: When memory constraints prevent using coupled methods.
  • Legacy code upgrade: Improving existing SIMPLE-based solvers with minimal effort.
  • Parametric studies: Multiple design iterations where convergence speed matters.

6. When NOT to Use SIMPLEC

Highly transient flows: Use PISO or PIMPLE for time-accurate simulations
Strong coupling scenarios: Natural convection with very high Rayleigh numbers
Research accuracy needs: When coupled solvers provide necessary precision
Simple geometries: SIMPLE may be sufficient for basic pipe/channel flows
Memory abundance: When coupled solvers are feasible and provide better performance

7. Setup and Guidelines

Solver Settings:

  • Under-relaxation factors: Pressure 0.3, Momentum 0.8-0.9, higher than SIMPLE.
  • Discretization: Second-order upwind for momentum, PRESTO! for pressure.
  • Convergence: Residuals < 10⁻⁴, monitor mass flow balance for validation.
  • Initialization: Use SIMPLE results or potential flow for better starting point.

Mesh Requirements:

  • Quality standards: Same as SIMPLE, skewness < 0.95, aspect ratio < 100.
  • Refinement strategy: Focus on pressure gradient regions for optimal performance.
  • Growth ratio: < 1.3 in critical flow regions, can be relaxed elsewhere.
  • Boundary layers: Adequate resolution for wall functions or low-Re models.

Boundary Conditions:

  • Inlet specification: Velocity inlet or mass flow inlet with proper profiles.
  • Outlet treatment: Pressure outlet for better pressure-velocity coupling.
  • Wall conditions: No-slip walls with appropriate wall function treatment.
  • Reference pressure: Set reference pressure point for closed domains.

Historical Context and Development

The SIMPLEC algorithm was developed by J.P. Van Doormaal and G.D. Raithby at the University of Waterloo in 1984 as a systematic improvement to the SIMPLE algorithm. The development addressed a fundamental inconsistency in SIMPLE's velocity correction formula, which omitted certain terms that could improve convergence behavior.

The Consistency Problem

Van Doormaal and Raithby identified that SIMPLE's velocity correction formula neglected neighbor contributions in the velocity correction step. This omission, while maintaining stability, resulted in slower convergence than theoretically possible. SIMPLEC addressed this by including previously omitted terms in a consistent manner.

The development of SIMPLEC marked an important milestone in segregated solver evolution, demonstrating that systematic analysis of existing algorithms could yield significant improvements without fundamental algorithmic changes. This approach influenced subsequent developments in pressure-velocity coupling methods.

Mathematical Foundation and Complete Derivation

SIMPLEC modifies the SIMPLE velocity correction formula to include previously omitted neighbor contributions, resulting in improved momentum-pressure coupling and faster convergence.

Standard SIMPLE Velocity Correction:

$$\mathbf{u}^{n+1} = \mathbf{u}^* - \frac{1}{a_p}\nabla p'$$

SIMPLE omits neighbor velocity corrections, leading to inconsistent momentum-pressure coupling.

SIMPLEC Velocity Correction:

$$\mathbf{u}^{n+1} = \mathbf{u}^* - \frac{1}{a_p - \sum a_{nb}}\nabla p'$$

SIMPLEC includes neighbor coefficients in the denominator, providing more consistent coupling.

Pressure Correction Equation:

$$\nabla \cdot \left( \frac{\rho}{a_p - \sum a_{nb}} \nabla p' \right) = \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}^*)$$

The pressure correction equation is modified accordingly to maintain consistency.

Variable Definitions:

ap: Diagonal coefficient in momentum equation
anb: Neighbor coefficients in momentum equation
p': Pressure correction field
u*: Predicted velocity field
Parameter Typical Value Description
αp 0.3 Pressure under-relaxation factor
αu 0.8-0.9 Enhanced momentum under-relaxation

Theoretical Analysis and Convergence Behavior

Consistency Analysis

The key insight of SIMPLEC lies in recognizing that the SIMPLE velocity correction formula is inconsistent with the momentum equation discretization. By including neighbor contributions that were previously omitted, SIMPLEC achieves better momentum-pressure coupling.

Convergence Characteristics

SIMPLEC typically demonstrates 20-30% faster convergence than SIMPLE due to the improved velocity correction formula. The algorithm maintains the stability characteristics of SIMPLE while enabling higher under-relaxation factors for momentum equations.

Spectral Analysis

Fourier analysis of SIMPLEC shows improved damping of high-frequency errors compared to SIMPLE, resulting in more efficient error reduction per iteration. The enhanced coupling reduces the spectral radius of the iteration matrix, leading to faster convergence.

Implementation and Practical Considerations

Computational Overhead

SIMPLEC requires minimal additional computational effort compared to SIMPLE. The main overhead comes from computing and storing the neighbor coefficient sums, which typically adds less than 5% to the total computational cost per iteration.

Memory Requirements

Memory requirements are slightly higher than SIMPLE due to the need to store additional coefficient information. However, this increase is typically less than 10% and is easily justified by the convergence improvement.

Numerical Stability

SIMPLEC maintains the robust stability characteristics of SIMPLE while allowing higher momentum under-relaxation factors. The algorithm is particularly effective for problems with moderate pressure-velocity coupling strength.

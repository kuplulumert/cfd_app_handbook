Conservation Law

Bernoulli Equation

Energy is conserved along a streamline in flowing fluids

\frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + z = \text{constant}

Valid for: Steady flow, incompressible fluids, no viscosity effects

What Each Symbol Means

p

Pressure

Pascals (Pa)

The push that the fluid exerts on everything around it

Example: 101,325 Pa (atmospheric pressure)

ρ

Density

kg/m³

How much stuff is packed into a given space

Example: 1000 kg/m³ (water)

g

Gravity

m/s²

How fast things fall due to Earth's pull

Example: 9.81 m/s² (standard gravity)

v

Velocity

m/s

How fast the fluid is moving

Example: 5 m/s (fast walking speed)

z

Height

m

How high above your reference point

Example: 10 m (3-story building)

💰 Think of it Like a Bank Account

Bernoulli's equation is like managing your energy budget. You have a total amount of energy that never changes, but you can move it between different "accounts."

🏦

Pressure Account (p/ρg)

Your checking account - energy you can spend right now. High pressure means lots of available energy.

🚗

Speed Account (v²/2g)

Your investment account - energy tied up in motion. Going faster means more energy invested in movement.

⛰️

Height Account (z)

Your savings account - energy stored for later. Being higher up means more potential energy saved.

💡 Key Insight: Your total balance stays the same! You can move money between accounts, but you can't create or destroy energy.

Real Example: Water Tank

🚰 Water Shooting Out of a Tank

📝 What We Know

  • • Tank height: 5 meters
  • • Water at top barely moving
  • • Both top and bottom at atmospheric pressure
  • • Want to find: How fast does water shoot out?

🧮 The Solution

Since pressure is the same everywhere and the top is nearly still, all the height energy converts to speed energy:

v = √(2gh) = 9.9 m/s

That's about 22 mph - like a garden hose on full blast!

When Bernoulli Lies to You

Bernoulli assumes perfect, frictionless flow. In the real world, it breaks down when:

🌪️ Viscous Effects

When fluid sticks to walls or has internal friction (like honey vs water)

⚡ High Speed Flow

When flow gets close to the speed of sound (compressible effects kick in)

🔄 Pumps & Turbines

When machines add or remove energy from the flow

⏱️ Unsteady Flow

When things change rapidly with time (like water hammer)

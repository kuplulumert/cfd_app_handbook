Bernoulli Equation

Energy per unit weight is conserved along a streamline in ideal fluid flow

fundamental
steady flow incompressible fluid inviscid flow along a streamline

Validity Range

Re min: 1,000
Ma max: 0.3

Valid for flows where viscous effects are negligible. Breaks down in boundary layers and highly viscous flows.

Equation

\frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + z = \text{constant}

Alternative forms:

p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g z = \text{constant}

Symbol Definitions

p

pressure

Units: Pa

Static pressure in the fluid

Typical: 101,325 Pa (atmospheric)

ρ

density

Units: kg/m³

Fluid density, assumed constant

Typical: 998 kg/m³ (water)

v

velocity

Units: m/s

Fluid velocity magnitude

Typical: 0.1-10 m/s

z

elevation

Units: m

Height above reference datum

Typical: 0-100 m

Analogy

💰

Bank Account for Energy per Unit Weight

Think of each term as a different form of "energy currency" in your bank account. Pressure head (p/ρg) is like cash in your checking account - readily available energy. Velocity head (v²/2g) is like an investment that yields kinetic energy - the faster you go, the more you have invested in motion. Elevation head (z) is like a savings account at altitude - potential energy stored for later use.

p/ρg → pressure energy (checking account)

v²/2g → kinetic energy (investment account)

z → potential energy (savings account)

When It Lies

Strong viscosity effects (boundary layers, pipe friction, flow separation)

Compressible flow at high Mach numbers (Ma > 0.3)

Across pumps, turbines, or other work-adding/removing devices

Worked Example

Water Tank to Jet (Torricelli's Law)

Water flows from a large tank through a small opening at the bottom. Calculate exit velocity.

Given:

h = 5 m (height difference)

p₁ = p₂ = atmospheric

v₁ ≈ 0 m/s (large tank)

Result:

v₂ = 9.9 m/s

Exit velocity depends only on height, like free fall!

Key Steps:

  1. Apply Bernoulli between tank surface and exit
  2. Since p₁ = p₂ and v₁ ≈ 0, equation simplifies to: h = v₂²/2g
  3. Solve: v₂ = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 5) = 9.9 m/s

Bernoulli Equation

Energy per unit weight is conserved along a streamline in ideal fluid flow

\frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{v^2}{2g} + z = \text{constant}
Fundamental Steady Flow Incompressible Inviscid

Valid when: Re > 1,000, Ma < 0.3, viscous effects negligible

What Each Symbol Means

p

Pressure

Pa (Pascals)

The force per unit area that the fluid exerts

Example: 101,325 Pa (atmospheric pressure)

ρ

Density

kg/m³

How much mass is packed into a given volume

Example: 998 kg/m³ (water at 20°C)

v

Velocity

m/s

How fast the fluid is moving

Example: 5 m/s (typical pipe flow)

z

Elevation

m

Height above your chosen reference point

Example: 10 m above ground level

The Bank Account Analogy

Think of Bernoulli's equation like a bank account for energy. Your total balance must always stay the same (conservation), but you can move money between different accounts:

🏦

Pressure Head (p/ρg)

Your checking account - readily available energy you can spend immediately

🚗

Velocity Head (v²/2g)

Your investment account - energy tied up in motion, earning kinetic returns

⛰️

Elevation Head (z)

Your savings account - potential energy stored at altitude for later use

💡 Key Insight: You can transfer money between accounts, but your total balance stays constant (unless you add energy with a pump or lose it to friction)

Real Example: Water Tank

🚰 Water Tank to Jet (Torricelli's Law)

📝 Given:

  • h = 5 m (height difference)
  • p₁ = p₂ = atmospheric pressure
  • v₁ ≈ 0 (large tank, slow surface)

✅ Answer:

v₂ = 9.9 m/s

Exit velocity depends only on height!

🧮 How we solved it:

  1. Since pressures are equal and v₁ ≈ 0, Bernoulli simplifies to: h = v₂²/2g
  2. Solve for v₂: v₂ = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 5)
  3. Calculate: v₂ = √98.1 = 9.9 m/s

🎯 CFD Guidance - k-kL-ω Transition Model

✅ When to Use k-kL-ω (Critical Applications)

  • Natural transition prediction - Tollmien-Schlichting wave instability growth
  • Low-pressure turbine blades - Designed specifically for these applications
  • Bypass transition flows - Freestream turbulence-induced transition
  • Research applications - When transition physics understanding is critical
  • Airfoil transition studies - Precise transition location prediction
  • Low Reynolds number flows - Where transition effects dominate
  • Heat transfer with transition - When transition affects thermal performance
  • Academic validation - Benchmark cases for transition modeling

⚠️ When to Avoid k-kL-ω (Critical Limitations)

  • Industrial routine analysis - Too complex and expensive for everyday use
  • High turbulence environments - Bypass transition dominates, model overkill
  • Limited computational resources - Three equations = higher cost than k-ω SST
  • Fully turbulent flows - No transition effects to capture
  • Separated flows - Transition less important than separation physics
  • Preliminary design studies - Too detailed for initial analysis
  • Large-scale simulations - Computational cost prohibitive
  • Convergence challenges - More difficult than standard RANS models

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Grid requirements: Very fine grids needed, y+ < 1 essential for transition capture
  • Initialization: Initialize k, kL, and ω carefully based on freestream conditions
  • Boundary conditions: Specify turbulence intensity and length scale at inlets
  • Convergence: Monitor all three equation residuals, use relaxation factors
  • Transition criteria: Model uses local variables to detect transition onset
  • Validation: Compare transition location with experimental data

📐 Transport Equations - Detailed Analysis

1️⃣ Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k) Equation

\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} =
P_k - \beta^* k \omega + R_{BP} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_k \nu_t)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Turbulent kinetic energy with bypass transition

∂k/∂t + Uⱼ ∂k/∂xⱼ: Unsteady + convection terms

Pk: Production by mean shear

-β*kω: Dissipation to small scales

RBP: Bypass transition source term (key addition)

Diffusion: (ν + σₖνₜ) ∂²k/∂xⱼ²

2️⃣ Laminar Kinetic Energy (kL) Equation

\frac{\partial k_L}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k_L}{\partial x_j} =
P_{k_L} - R_{BP} - R_{NAT} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\nu \frac{\partial k_L}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Pre-transition fluctuation energy (laminar kinetic energy)

∂kL/∂t + Uⱼ ∂kL/∂xⱼ: Transport of laminar fluctuations

PkL: Production of laminar fluctuations by instabilities

-RBP: Transfer to turbulent k (bypass transition)

-RNAT: Transfer to turbulent k (natural transition)

Diffusion: Only molecular diffusion (ν ∂²kL/∂xⱼ²)

3️⃣ Specific Dissipation Rate (ω) Equation

\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j} =
\alpha \frac{\omega}{k} P_k - \beta \omega^2 + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_\omega \nu_t)\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Length scale equation (similar to standard k-ω)

∂ω/∂t + Uⱼ ∂ω/∂xⱼ: Transport of specific dissipation rate

α (ω/k)Pk: Production proportional to k production

-β ω²: Destruction of ω

Diffusion: (ν + σωνₜ) ∂²ω/∂xⱼ²

4️⃣ Transition Source Terms

R_{BP} = C_{BP} \max(0, \alpha_{BP} - \alpha_{BP0}) k_L
R_{NAT} = C_{NAT} f_{NAT} k_L \omega

Physical Meaning: Controls transfer from laminar to turbulent energy

RBP: Bypass transition source (high freestream turbulence)

RNAT: Natural transition source (Tollmien-Schlichting waves)

αBP: Bypass parameter based on pressure gradient

fNAT: Natural transition function

💡 Transition Process Analogy

Think of k-kL-ω as modeling the transformation from ordered to chaotic motion - like a calm river gradually becoming turbulent rapids.

Laminar kinetic energy (kL): Like small ripples on a calm river - organized disturbances that grow slowly.

Natural transition: Like rocks in the riverbed causing specific wave patterns (Tollmien-Schlichting waves) that gradually amplify until the flow becomes chaotic.

Bypass transition: Like throwing stones into the river - external disturbances (freestream turbulence) that directly trigger chaos without the gradual wave growth.

Turbulent kinetic energy (k): The fully chaotic motion after transition - like the whitewater rapids downstream.

Transfer terms (RBP, RNAT): The mechanisms that convert organized ripples into chaotic turbulence - the "tipping point" where order becomes disorder.

🔬 Transition Physics

Natural Transition (Low Tu)

Tollmien-Schlichting waves grow exponentially in adverse pressure gradients until nonlinear breakdown occurs. Model tracks this through kL growth and RNAT transfer mechanism.

Bypass Transition (High Tu)

Freestream turbulence penetrates boundary layer, creating streamwise streaks that break down directly to turbulence. Model captures this through RBP source term.

Transition Detection

Model automatically detects transition onset based on local pressure gradient, freestream turbulence, and boundary layer parameters. No user-specified transition location needed.

k-kL

k-kL-ω Transition

Three-equation transition model - Specialized for automatic transition prediction

✅
Use k-kL-ω When (Industry Focus)
Situation Why It Works Advice
Transition-critical flows (airfoils, turbine blades) Automatically predicts transition location Use when transition location affects performance
Low-pressure turbines (aerospace applications) Designed specifically for LPT transition physics Standard choice for low-pressure turbine design
Natural transition flows (smooth surfaces) Handles both natural and bypass transition Good for smooth airfoil applications
Research transition studies Physics-based transition criteria Excellent for understanding transition physics
Moderate Re flows (Re: 10⁴-10⁶) Covers the transition Re range effectively Perfect for moderate Re applications
⚠️
Avoid k-kL-ω When (Industry Focus)
Situation Why It Fails Industry Advice
Fully turbulent flows (Re > 10⁶) Overkill, no transition occurs Use k-ω SST for fully turbulent flows
Industrial equipment (pumps, fans, HVAC) Too specialized, higher computational cost Use k-ε for industrial applications
Fully laminar flows (Re < 1,000) No transition expected, unnecessary complexity Use laminar model for low Re flows
Rough surface flows (industrial applications) Designed for smooth surfaces, roughness triggers transition Use turbulent models for rough surfaces
Large-scale simulations (computational cost) 3 equations more expensive than 2-equation models Use simpler models for large systems
Engineering design iterations (fast results needed) Slower convergence, more complex setup Use k-ω SST for design iterations

Transport Equations
Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k)
$$\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} = P_k - \beta^* k \omega + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_k \nu_t)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]$$
Laminar Kinetic Energy (kL)
$$\frac{\partial k_L}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k_L}{\partial x_j} = P_{kL} - D_L + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\nu \frac{\partial k_L}{\partial x_j}\right]$$

Tracks pre-transitional fluctuations

Specific Dissipation Rate (ω)
$$\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j} = \alpha \frac{\omega}{k} P_k - \beta \omega^2 + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_\omega \nu_t)\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\right]$$

Engineering Analogy

🌡️ Smart Thermostat with Prediction

k-kL-ω is like a smart thermostat that predicts when to switch heating modes:

  • kL (pre-heating): "Tracks small fluctuations before full heating kicks in"
  • k (full heating): "Main turbulent energy once transition happens"
  • ω (frequency): "Controls how fast the energy cycles"
  • Automatic detection: "Knows when to switch from pre-heat to full heat"
  • Specialized purpose: "Perfect for transition-sensitive applications"

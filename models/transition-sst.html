🎯 CFD Guidance - Transition SST Model

✅ When to Use Transition SST (Critical Applications)

  • Industrial turbomachinery - Turbines, compressors, fans where transition matters
  • Airfoil design and optimization - When transition location affects performance
  • Heat transfer applications - Transition significantly affects heat transfer rates
  • Automotive aerodynamics - External flows with transition on body surfaces
  • Wind energy applications - Wind turbine blades with varying transition
  • Commercial CFD analysis - Industry standard for transition prediction
  • Design optimization - When transition location is a design parameter
  • Validation studies - Comparing with experimental transition data

⚠️ When to Avoid Transition SST (Critical Limitations)

  • Fully turbulent flows - No transition effects present, unnecessary complexity
  • Preliminary design studies - Too detailed for initial conceptual analysis
  • Large-scale simulations - Four equations increase computational cost significantly
  • Simple internal flows - Transition less critical in fully developed flows
  • Limited computational resources - Higher memory and CPU requirements
  • Routine industrial analysis - k-ω SST often sufficient for many applications
  • Separated flow dominated cases - Separation physics more important than transition
  • Convergence difficulties - More equations = more potential convergence issues

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Grid requirements: y+ < 1 at walls essential for accurate transition capture
  • Initialization: Initialize k, ω, γ, and Reθt based on freestream conditions
  • Boundary conditions: Specify turbulence intensity at inlets, γ = 1 at walls
  • Convergence: Monitor all four equation residuals, use under-relaxation
  • Transition validation: Compare predicted transition location with experiments
  • Intermittency interpretation: γ = 0 (laminar), γ = 1 (turbulent)

📐 Transport Equations - Detailed Analysis

1️⃣ Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k) Equation

\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} =
\gamma P_k - \beta^* k \omega + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_k \nu_t)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Turbulent kinetic energy with intermittency effects

∂k/∂t + Uⱼ ∂k/∂xⱼ: Unsteady + convection terms

γ Pk: Production modulated by intermittency (γ = 0 in laminar, γ = 1 in turbulent)

-β*kω: Dissipation term, β* = 0.09

Diffusion: (ν + σₖνₜ) ∂²k/∂xⱼ²

2️⃣ Specific Dissipation Rate (ω) Equation

\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j} =
\gamma \alpha \frac{\omega}{k} P_k - \beta \omega^2 + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_\omega \nu_t)\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Specific dissipation rate with intermittency effects

∂ω/∂t + Uⱼ ∂ω/∂xⱼ: Transport of specific dissipation rate

γ α (ω/k)Pk: Production modulated by intermittency

-β ω²: Destruction of ω

Diffusion: (ν + σωνₜ) ∂²ω/∂xⱼ²

3️⃣ Intermittency (γ) Equation

\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial x_j} =
P_{\gamma} - E_{\gamma} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_{\gamma}})\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Fraction of time flow is turbulent (0 = laminar, 1 = turbulent)

∂γ/∂t + Uⱼ ∂γ/∂xⱼ: Transport of intermittency

Pγ: Production of intermittency (transition onset)

-Eγ: Destruction of intermittency (relaminarization)

Diffusion: (ν + νₜ/σγ) ∂²γ/∂xⱼ²

4️⃣ Transition Momentum Thickness Reynolds Number (Reθt)

\frac{\partial \tilde{Re}_{\theta t}}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \tilde{Re}_{\theta t}}{\partial x_j} =
P_{\theta t} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[2(\nu + \nu_t)\frac{\partial \tilde{Re}_{\theta t}}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Critical Reynolds number for transition onset

∂Reθt/∂t + Uⱼ ∂Reθt/∂xⱼ: Transport of transition criterion

Pθt: Source term based on local flow conditions

Diffusion: Enhanced diffusion (factor of 2)

Purpose: Provides transition criterion based on local pressure gradient and turbulence

💡 Intermittency-Based Analogy

Think of Transition SST as modeling the gradual switch from laminar to turbulent flow - like a dimmer switch that gradually turns on turbulence instead of an instant on/off switch.

Intermittency (γ): Like the dimmer setting - γ = 0 means "turbulence off" (fully laminar), γ = 1 means "turbulence fully on" (fully turbulent), and values in between represent partial turbulence.

Transition Reynolds number (Reθt): Like the voltage threshold needed to turn on the dimmer - it depends on local conditions (pressure gradient, surface roughness, freestream turbulence).

Production terms (Pγ, Pθt): Like sensors that detect when conditions are right to start turning up the dimmer switch.

k-ω SST base: The underlying turbulence model that gets "dimmed" by the intermittency - like the light bulb that gets controlled by the dimmer switch.

Advantage: Smooth transition instead of abrupt change - like gradually increasing light instead of sudden on/off.

🔬 Transition Physics & Correlations

Intermittency Concept

γ represents the fraction of time the flow is turbulent at a given location. In the transition region, γ varies from 0 to 1, providing smooth transition between laminar and turbulent states.

Correlation-Based Approach

Model uses empirical correlations from extensive experimental databases to predict transition onset based on local pressure gradient, freestream turbulence, and Reynolds number.

Industrial Robustness

Designed for industrial applications with robust convergence properties and extensive validation in turbomachinery applications. More practical than research-oriented models.

  • More complex setup and boundary conditions
  • Additional convergence monitoring required
  • Correlation-based (less fundamental than k-kL-ω)
  • Limited to calibrated flow types

k-ε Model

Industrial workhorse for high Reynolds number turbulent flows

When to Use
Ideal situations
  • High Re flows (Re > 10,000) - fully turbulent
  • Industrial equipment - pumps, fans, HVAC systems
  • Engineering accuracy - ±10-20% sufficient
  • Fast results - tight deadlines, design iterations
  • Far from walls - y⁺ > 30 acceptable
When to Avoid
Problem situations
  • Low Re flows (Re < 2,000) - use laminar
  • Near-wall accuracy - heat transfer critical
  • Flow separation - use k-ω SST instead
  • High precision - <5% error needed
  • Rotating flows - mixers, cyclones
Bottom Line
Use k-ε as your default when speed, robustness, and "good-enough" accuracy matter more than precision. Switch to k-ω SST when walls, separation, or transitional regions are critical. Perfect for industrial applications where fast turnaround beats research-level detail.
Common Applications
HVAC Systems
Airflow in buildings, ductwork
Industrial Equipment
Pumps, fans, mixing tanks
Automotive
Engine cooling, HVAC design
Process Engineering
Chemical reactors, piping systems
Transport Equations
Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k)
$$\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} = P_k - \varepsilon + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_k})\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]$$
Dissipation Rate (ε)
$$\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j} = C_{1\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon}{k} P_k - C_{2\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon^2}{k} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_\varepsilon})\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j}\right]$$

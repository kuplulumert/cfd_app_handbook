k-ε Model

Industrial workhorse for high Reynolds number turbulent flows

When to Use
Quick decisions
  • High Re flows (Re > 10,000) - fully turbulent
  • Refrigerator cooling - internal air circulation
  • Oven air flow - convection heating analysis
  • Engineering accuracy - ±10-20% sufficient
  • Fast results - tight deadlines, design iterations
When to Avoid
Problem situations
  • Low Re flows (Re < 2,000) - use laminar
  • Heat pump heat exchangers - near-wall accuracy critical
  • Washing machine drum - complex rotating flow
  • High precision - <5% error needed
  • Dishwasher spray arms - flow separation expected
Bottom Line for Home Appliances
Use k-ε as your default for most Beko appliance flows like refrigerator cooling systems, oven circulation, and HVAC ducting. Switch to k-ω SST for heat pumps, washing machine drums, or any application where precise heat transfer matters. Perfect for preliminary design and feasibility studies where fast turnaround beats research-level detail.

Industry Focus
Situation Why It Works Advice
High Re, fully turbulent flows (Re > 10,000) k-ε is tuned for industrial turbulence Don't waste time resolving laminar/turbulent transitions
Far from walls (ducts, pipes, HVAC) Wall functions are acceptable Keep y⁺ > 30 and avoid fine near-wall meshing

Avoid k-ε When
Industry Focus
Situation Why It Fails Industry Advice
Heat pump evaporators/condensers Near-wall heat transfer accuracy critical Use k-ω SST with y⁺ < 1 for precise heat transfer
Washing machine drum flow Complex rotating, swirling flow patterns Use Reynolds Stress Model or k-ω SST
Dishwasher spray arms Flow separation and complex jet interactions Use k-ω SST for better separation prediction
Low Re or transitional flows (Re < 2,000) Assumes fully turbulent Use k-ω SST or transitional models
Microwave cavity flows Low Reynolds number, complex geometry Consider laminar or low-Re models
High precision required (<5% error, research cases) Too crude for detailed studies LES, DES, or advanced RANS needed
Industry Takeaway
Default Choice for Appliances
Use standard k-ε as your default for Beko refrigerators, ovens, and HVAC systems when speed, robustness, and "good-enough" accuracy are the priority.
Upgrade for Heat Transfer
Switch to k-ω SST for heat pumps, condensers, or any application where precise wall heat transfer matters.
High Fidelity for Complex Flows
Go higher fidelity (LES, DES, RSM) only for washing machines, complex rotating flows, or when precision is worth the cost.
Key Equations
Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k)
$$\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} = P_k - \varepsilon + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_k}\right) \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]$$
Dissipation Rate (ε)
$$\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j} = C_{1\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon}{k} P_k - C_{2\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon^2}{k} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_\varepsilon}\right) \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j}\right]$$
Turbulent Viscosity
$$\nu_t = C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\varepsilon}$$
Engineering Analogy
Refrigerator Compressor System
k-ε is like managing a refrigerator's compressor and cooling system:
  • k (energy): "How much cooling power the compressor provides"
  • ε (dissipation): "How fast cooling energy gets used up by heat loads"
  • Balance: "More cooling input → better performance, but also more energy consumption"
  • Limitation: "Works great for standard cooling, struggles with precision temperature control in specific zones"

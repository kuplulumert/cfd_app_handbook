k-ε Model

Industrial workhorse for high Reynolds number turbulent flows

Use k-ε When
Industry Focus
Situation Why It Works Advice
High Re, fully turbulent flows (Re > 10,000) k-ε is tuned for industrial turbulence Don't waste time resolving laminar/turbulent transitions
Far from walls (ducts, pipes, HVAC) Wall functions are acceptable Keep y⁺ > 30 and avoid fine near-wall meshing
Common equipment (pumps, fans, HVAC, appliances) Matches empirical data, widely validated Use it for design iterations and quick feasibility
Engineering accuracy is enough (±10–20%) Fast turnaround > precision Good for sizing, feasibility, or system-level trade-offs
Tight deadlines / fast results needed Robust and stable convergence Default choice when project delivery > research detail
Avoid k-ε When
Industry Focus
Situation Why It Fails Industry Advice
Low Re or transitional flows (Re < 2,000) Assumes fully turbulent Use k-ω SST or transitional models
Near-wall accuracy needed (heat transfer, fouling, thin films) Wall functions break down Go for low-Re k-ε or k-ω SST
Flow separation expected (diffusers, curved ducts, aero surfaces) Poor at predicting separation Use k-ω SST or hybrid RANS/LES
Swirling or rotating flows (mixers, cyclones, turbines) Weak in strong rotation Try RNG k-ε or Reynolds Stress Model
High precision required (<5% error, research cases) Too crude for detailed studies LES, DES, or advanced RANS needed
Unsteady/time-dependent flows (pulsation, combustion, acoustics) k-ε assumes steady averaging Use LES/DES if time resolution is critical
Industry Takeaway
Default Choice
Use standard k-ε as your default when speed, robustness, and "good-enough" accuracy are the priority.
Upgrade When Needed
Switch to k-ω SST when walls, separation, or transitional regions matter.
High Fidelity
Go higher fidelity (LES, DES, RSM) only when precision is worth the cost.

Key Equations
Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k)
$$\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} = P_k - \varepsilon + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_k}\right) \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]$$
Dissipation Rate (ε)
$$\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j} = C_{1\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon}{k} P_k - C_{2\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon^2}{k} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_\varepsilon}\right) \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j}\right]$$
Turbulent Viscosity
$$\nu_t = C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\varepsilon}$$
Engineering Analogy
Factory Production Line
k-ε is like managing a busy factory production line:
  • k (energy): "How much activity/energy the workers have"
  • ε (dissipation): "How fast energy gets used up by friction"
  • Balance: "More energy input → more production, but also more energy loss"
  • Limitation: "Works great for standard operations, struggles with unusual situations"

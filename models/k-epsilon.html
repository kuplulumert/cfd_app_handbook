k-ε Model

Industrial workhorse for high Reynolds number turbulent flows

1. Quick Overview

  • • Standard two-equation RANS model (solves k and ε).
  • • Assumes flow is fully turbulent.
  • • Most widely used in industrial CFD.

2. Strengths

  • • Robust, stable, and computationally inexpensive.
  • • Handles high Reynolds number, fully developed turbulence well.
  • • Supported in every commercial CFD solver.
  • • Excellent for early design studies and quick iterations.

3. Limitations

  • • Poor near-wall accuracy (needs wall functions, y⁺ > 30).
  • • Struggles with laminar–turbulent transition.
  • • Weak in separation, recirculation, swirling flows.
  • • Not suitable for low Reynolds number cases.

4. Industry Use Cases (Beko Focus)

Refrigerators — airflow inside cabinet
Ovens — hot air circulation
Air conditioners — duct and diffuser flows
General appliances — feasibility studies, fast prototypes

5. When Not to Use

Washing machines & dishwashers (need near-wall detail → SST better)
Vacuum cleaners with strong swirl/curvature (use RSM or SST + correction)
Research cases requiring < 5% accuracy

6. Practical CFD Tips

  • • Use standard wall functions with y⁺ > 30.
  • • For duct flows, check Re > 10,000 for validity.
  • • Start with first-order discretization for stability, then switch to second-order.
  • • Acceptable for ±10–20% engineering accuracy.

Historical Context and Development

The k-ε model was developed by Launder and Spalding (1972-1974) based on earlier work by Jones and Launder (1971). It represents the first complete closure for the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes equations using the eddy viscosity hypothesis.

Timeline and Evolution:

  • • 1925: Prandtl introduces mixing length concept
  • • 1945: Kolmogorov proposes k-ε model foundation
  • • 1971: Jones & Launder develop early two-equation models
  • • 1974: Launder & Spalding establish standard k-ε model
  • • 1992: Yakhot & Orszag introduce RNG k-ε variant
  • • 1995: Shih et al. develop Realizable k-ε model

Mathematical Foundation and Derivation

The k-ε model emerges from the exact Reynolds stress transport equations through systematic modeling approximations. Starting from the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations:

Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes Equations:

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho \overline{u_i})}{\partial x_i} = 0$$ $$\frac{\partial(\rho \overline{u_i})}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho \overline{u_i u_j})}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{\partial \overline{p}}{\partial x_i} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\mu\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right) - \rho\overline{u_i'u_j'}\right]$$

Exact Reynolds Stress Transport Equation:

$$\frac{D(\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'})}{Dt} = P_{ij} + \Phi_{ij} - \epsilon_{ij} + D_{ij}^T + D_{ij}^L + D_{ij}^M$$

Where Pij = Production, Φij = Pressure-strain, εij = Dissipation, Dij = Diffusion terms

Turbulent Kinetic Energy Transport Equation (k = ½τii):

$$\frac{\partial(\rho k)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho k u_i)}{\partial x_i} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\mu + \frac{\mu_t}{\sigma_k}\right) \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right] + G_k + G_b - \rho\epsilon - Y_M$$

Dissipation Rate Transport Equation (Modeled):

$$\frac{\partial(\rho \epsilon)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho \epsilon u_i)}{\partial x_i} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\mu + \frac{\mu_t}{\sigma_\epsilon}\right) \frac{\partial \epsilon}{\partial x_j}\right] + C_{1\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{k}(G_k + C_{3\epsilon}G_b) - C_{2\epsilon}\rho\frac{\epsilon^2}{k}$$

Turbulent Viscosity (Dimensional Analysis + Kolmogorov Hypothesis):

$$\mu_t = \rho C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\epsilon}, \quad \text{where } C_\mu = f\left(\frac{k^2/\epsilon^2}{S^2}\right) \approx 0.09$$

Derivation of ε-equation Structure:

The ε-equation is not exact but follows from dimensional analysis and the requirement that:

$$\epsilon \sim \frac{k^{3/2}}{\ell}, \quad \text{where } \ell \text{ is integral length scale}$$

Comprehensive Term Definitions and Physical Meaning

k: Turbulent kinetic energy per unit mass [m²/s²] $$k = \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'} = \frac{1}{2}(\overline{u'^2} + \overline{v'^2} + \overline{w'^2})$$

Physical meaning: Represents the average kinetic energy contained in turbulent fluctuations. In isotropic turbulence, k scales with mean square velocity fluctuations.

ε: Turbulent dissipation rate [m²/s³] $$\epsilon = \nu \overline{\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}} = 2\nu \overline{s_{ij}'s_{ij}'}$$

Physical meaning: Rate at which turbulent kinetic energy is converted to internal energy through viscous action at the smallest scales (Kolmogorov microscale).

μₜ: Turbulent (eddy) viscosity [kg/m·s] $$\mu_t = \rho C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\epsilon} = \rho \nu_t$$

Physical meaning: Represents enhanced momentum transport due to turbulent mixing. Typically 10²-10⁵ times larger than molecular viscosity in high-Re flows.

Gₖ: Shear production of turbulent kinetic energy $$G_k = -\rho \overline{u_i'u_j'} \frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x_i} = \mu_t S^2 = \mu_t \left(2\overline{S_{ij}S_{ij}}\right)$$

Physical meaning: Energy transfer from mean flow to turbulent fluctuations through Reynolds stress work. Always positive (energy source).

Gᵦ: Buoyant production/destruction of turbulent kinetic energy $$G_b = \beta g_i \frac{\mu_t}{Pr_t} \frac{\partial T}{\partial x_i}, \quad \beta = -\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial T}\right)_p$$

Physical meaning: Gb > 0 for unstable stratification (buoyancy enhances turbulence), Gb < 0 for stable stratification (buoyancy suppresses turbulence).

S: Magnitude of mean strain rate tensor $$S = \sqrt{2S_{ij}S_{ij}}, \quad S_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right)$$

Physical meaning: Measure of mean flow deformation rate. In simple shear flow, S = |∂u/∂y|.

Yₘ: Dilatation dissipation in compressible flows $$Y_M = 2\rho \epsilon M_t^2, \quad M_t = \sqrt{\frac{k}{a^2}}$$

Physical meaning: Additional dissipation due to compressibility effects. Becomes significant when turbulent Mach number Mt > 0.25.

C₃ε: Buoyancy coupling parameter $$C_{3\epsilon} = \tanh\left|\frac{v}{u}\right|$$

Physical meaning: Accounts for principal flow direction in stratified flows. Ranges from 0 (horizontal flow) to 1 (vertical flow).

Turbulent Scales and Energy Cascade

Kolmogorov Microscales (Dissipation Scales):

$$\eta = \left(\frac{\nu^3}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/4}, \quad \tau_\eta = \left(\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/2}, \quad u_\eta = (\nu\epsilon)^{1/4}$$

Where η = length scale, τη = time scale, uη = velocity scale at which viscous dissipation occurs.

Integral Scales (Energy-Containing Scales):

$$\ell_t = C_\mu^{3/4}\frac{k^{3/2}}{\epsilon}, \quad \tau_t = \frac{k}{\epsilon}, \quad u_t = \sqrt{k}$$

Taylor Microscale (Inertial Subrange):

$$\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{15\nu k}{\epsilon}}, \quad Re_\lambda = \frac{u_t \lambda}{\nu} = \sqrt{15Re_t}$$

Scale Separation and Reynolds Number:

$$Re_t = \frac{k^2}{\nu\epsilon}, \quad \frac{\ell_t}{\eta} \sim Re_t^{3/4}, \quad \frac{\tau_t}{\tau_\eta} \sim Re_t^{1/2}$$

For the k-ε model to be valid, Ret >> 1 (typically Ret > 200) is required for clear scale separation.

Model Constants: Derivation and Physical Significance

$$C_\mu = 0.09, \quad C_{1\epsilon} = 1.44, \quad C_{2\epsilon} = 1.92$$ $$\sigma_k = 1.0, \quad \sigma_\epsilon = 1.3, \quad C_{3\epsilon} = \tanh\left|\frac{v}{u}\right|$$

Physical Basis for Constants:

Cμ = 0.09: Derived from log-law requirements in equilibrium boundary layers $$C_\mu = \left(\kappa^2\sqrt{C_{2\epsilon} - C_{1\epsilon}}\right)^{-1} = \left(0.41^2\sqrt{1.92-1.44}\right)^{-1} \approx 0.09$$
C1ε = 1.44: Ensures correct behavior in homogeneous shear flows $$C_{1\epsilon} = 1 + \frac{2}{3}A_1^{(h)} = 1 + \frac{2}{3}(0.66) \approx 1.44$$
C2ε = 1.92: Controls dissipation rate in isotropic turbulence decay $$C_{2\epsilon} = C_{1\epsilon} + \frac{\kappa^2}{\sqrt{C_\mu}} = 1.44 + \frac{0.41^2}{\sqrt{0.09}} \approx 1.92$$
σk = 1.0, σε = 1.3: Turbulent Prandtl numbers for k and ε transport

Based on analogy with heat transfer and calibration against boundary layer data.

Calibration Database (Historical Validation):

  • • Homogeneous shear flows: Pk/ε ≈ 1.8 (equilibrium ratio)
  • • Boundary layer flows: Skin friction coefficient Cf predictions
  • • Free jets: Spreading rate β = 0.094 (experimental: 0.094 ± 0.008)
  • • Mixing layers: Growth rate δω/x ≈ 0.18
  • • Isotropic decay: k/k0 = (t/t0)-n, n = 1.3

Realizability Constraints:

$$\overline{u_i'u_j'}\overline{u_i'u_j'} \geq 0, \quad \overline{u_i'u_i'} \geq 0, \quad -\frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'} \leq \overline{u_i'u_j'} \leq \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'}$$

Standard k-ε model can violate these constraints in strained flows, leading to unphysical results.

Advanced Theoretical Framework and Assumptions

Reynolds Decomposition and Averaging Operations:

$$u_i(x,t) = \overline{u_i}(x) + u_i'(x,t), \quad p(x,t) = \overline{p}(x) + p'(x,t)$$

Where overbar denotes ensemble/time-averaged quantities and prime denotes fluctuating components. For stationary turbulence:

$$\overline{u_i} = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \int_0^T u_i(x,t) dt, \quad \overline{u_i'} = 0$$

Generalized Boussinesq Hypothesis with Anisotropy:

$$-\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'} = \mu_t \left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right) - \frac{2}{3}\left(\rho k + \mu_t \frac{\partial \overline{u_k}}{\partial x_k}\right)\delta_{ij}$$

This assumes that Reynolds stresses align with mean strain rate (isotropic turbulent viscosity assumption).

Anisotropy Tensor and Model Limitations:

$$a_{ij} = \frac{\overline{u_i'u_j'}}{2k} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}, \quad \sum_{i} a_{ii} = 0$$

For isotropic turbulence, aij = 0. The k-ε model assumes local isotropy, which fails in:

  • • Strongly strained flows (a11 - a22 ≠ 0)
  • • Flows with streamline curvature
  • • Rotating reference frames

Critical Assumptions and Validity Range:

  • • Gradient diffusion hypothesis: ∇(turbulent transport) ∝ ∇(transported quantity)
  • • Local equilibrium hypothesis: Pk ≈ ε in logarithmic layer (∂Pk/∂t ≈ ∂ε/∂t ≈ 0)
  • • High Reynolds number: Ret = k²/(νε) >> 1, typically Ret > 200
  • • Turbulent time scale assumption: τt = k/ε represents characteristic eddy turnover time
  • • Kolmogorov hypothesis: Small scales are isotropic and universal

Modeling Approximations in ε-Equation:

The ε-equation is entirely modeled (not exact) based on:

$$\frac{D\epsilon}{Dt} \sim \frac{\epsilon}{k}P_k - \frac{\epsilon^2}{k} + \text{diffusion terms}$$

This structure ensures dimensional consistency and proper asymptotic behavior in simple flows.

Wall Treatment and Near-Wall Modeling

Standard Wall Functions:

The k-ε model requires wall functions due to its inability to integrate to the wall. The logarithmic law:

$$u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln(y^+) + B$$

Where κ = 0.41 (von Kármán constant), B = 5.2, and:

$$u^+ = \frac{u}{u_\tau}, \quad y^+ = \frac{y u_\tau}{\nu}, \quad u_\tau = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}}$$

Wall Function Requirements:

  • • y⁺ range: 30 < y⁺ < 300 for accurate results
  • • Boundary conditions at wall: k = 0, ε → ∞ (handled by wall functions)
  • • First cell center: Should be in logarithmic region

Model Limitations and Variants

Fundamental Limitations:

  • • Round-jet spreading rate: Over-predicts by ~20%
  • • Streamline curvature: Fails in swirling flows, cyclones
  • • Adverse pressure gradients: Poor separation prediction
  • • Impingement flows: Over-predicts heat transfer coefficients

Common Variants:

RNG k-ε: Renormalization Group theory-based constants $$C_\mu = 0.0845, \quad C_{1\epsilon} = 1.42, \quad C_{2\epsilon} = 1.68$$
Realizable k-ε: Ensures realizability conditions $$C_\mu = \frac{1}{A_0 + A_s \frac{kU^*}{\epsilon}}, \quad C_{2\epsilon} = 1.9$$

Turbulent Time and Length Scales:

$$\tau_t = \frac{k}{\epsilon}, \quad \ell_t = C_\mu^{3/4}\frac{k^{3/2}}{\epsilon}$$

Advanced Model Variants and Modern Improvements

RNG k-ε Model (Yakhot & Orszag, 1992):

Based on renormalization group theory, provides systematic derivation of model constants:

$$C_\mu = 0.0845, \quad C_{1\epsilon} = 1.42, \quad C_{2\epsilon} = 1.68$$ $$\sigma_k = \sigma_\epsilon = 0.7179$$
$$C_{2\epsilon}^* = C_{2\epsilon} + \frac{C_\mu \eta^3 (1-\eta/\eta_0)}{1 + \beta\eta^3}, \quad \eta = S\frac{k}{\epsilon}, \quad \eta_0 = 4.38, \quad \beta = 0.012$$

Realizable k-ε Model (Shih et al., 1995):

Ensures realizability conditions are satisfied, prevents unphysical normal stresses:

$$C_\mu = \frac{1}{A_0 + A_s \frac{kU^*}{\epsilon}}, \quad U^* = \sqrt{S_{ij}S_{ij} + \tilde{\Omega}_{ij}\tilde{\Omega}_{ij}}$$
$$A_0 = 4.04, \quad A_s = \sqrt{6}\cos\phi, \quad \phi = \frac{1}{3}\arccos(\sqrt{6}W), \quad W = \frac{S_{ij}S_{jk}S_{ki}}{S^3}$$

Low-Reynolds Number Extensions:

Launder-Sharma (1974) and Abe-Kondoh-Nagano (1994) modifications for near-wall integration:

$$C_\mu = C_\mu^* f_\mu, \quad f_\mu = \exp\left(-\frac{3.4}{(1+Re_t/50)^2}\right), \quad Re_t = \frac{k^2}{\nu\epsilon}$$

Rotation and Curvature Corrections:

$$C_\mu = C_\mu^* f_r, \quad f_r = \max\left[0, \frac{1-0.03\sqrt{\Omega k/\epsilon}}{1+0.23\sqrt{\Omega k/\epsilon}}\right]$$

Where Ω is the magnitude of mean rotation rate tensor.

Advanced Numerical Implementation and Stability

Source Term Linearization for Stability:

$$S_k = G_k - \rho\epsilon \rightarrow S_k = G_k - \rho\frac{\epsilon}{k}k = G_k - S_k^{impl} k$$
$$S_\epsilon = C_{1\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{k}G_k - C_{2\epsilon}\rho\frac{\epsilon^2}{k} \rightarrow C_{1\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{k}G_k - C_{2\epsilon}\rho\frac{\epsilon}{k}\epsilon$$

Realizability-Preserving Discretization:

  • • Production limiter: Pk = min(Pk, 10ρε) to prevent excessive production
  • • Lower bounds: kmin = 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s², εmin = 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s³
  • • Time scale limiting: τt = max(k/ε, C_τ√(ν/ε)), C_τ ≈ 6

Advanced Discretization Schemes:

QUICK scheme for convection: Third-order accuracy, reduces numerical diffusion $$\phi_f = \phi_P + \frac{r}{2}(\phi_E - \phi_P) - \frac{1-r}{8}(\phi_{EE} - 3\phi_E + 3\phi_P - \phi_W)$$
Central differencing with deferred correction: $$\nabla\phi|_f = \nabla\phi|_f^{CD} + \psi[\nabla\phi|_f^{HO} - \nabla\phi|_f^{CD}]$$

Convergence Acceleration Techniques:

  • • Multigrid methods: V-cycles for elliptic pressure equation
  • • Algebraic multigrid (AMG): For unstructured grids
  • • Block-coupled solvers: Solve momentum-k-ε simultaneously

Advanced Convergence Criteria:

$$\text{Scaled residuals: } \frac{|\sum_P a_P \phi_P - \sum_{nb} a_{nb} \phi_{nb} - S_P|}{\sum_P |a_P \phi_P|} < 10^{-4}$$ $$\text{Mass imbalance: } \frac{|\dot{m}_{in} - \dot{m}_{out}|}{|\dot{m}_{in}|} < 10^{-3}$$

Mesh Quality Requirements:

  • • Aspect ratio: AR < 100 in boundary layer regions
  • • Skewness: < 0.95 for tetrahedral cells
  • • Growth ratio: < 1.3 between adjacent cells

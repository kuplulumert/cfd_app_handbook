k-ε Model

Industrial workhorse for high Reynolds number turbulent flows

1. Model Overview

  • Two-equation RANS model solving for turbulent kinetic energy (k) and dissipation rate (ε).
  • First complete turbulence closure, developed by Launder & Spalding (1974).
  • Most widely validated and used model in industrial CFD applications.
  • Optimized for high Reynolds number, fully developed turbulent flows.

2. Key Advantages

  • Computational efficiency: Lowest cost among two-equation models, 40-50% faster than SST.
  • Robust convergence: Stable and reliable across wide range of flow conditions.
  • Universal availability: Implemented in every commercial CFD solver with consistent formulation.
  • Extensive validation: 50+ years of validation data across diverse industrial applications.
  • Mesh flexibility: Works with coarser meshes due to wall function approach.

3. Computational Requirements

  • Mesh requirements: y⁺ > 30 for wall functions, coarser near-wall mesh acceptable.
  • Wall treatment limitation: Cannot integrate to wall, requires empirical wall functions.
  • Reynolds number limitation: Re > 10,000 for validity, poor for transitional flows.
  • Separation issues: Over-predicts attached flow, under-predicts separation.

4. Beko Appliance Applications

Refrigerators & Freezers: Internal air circulation, compressor cooling, heat exchanger flows
Ovens & Cooktops: Hot air circulation, ventilation systems, simple heating applications
Air Conditioners: Duct flows, heat exchanger performance, fan-driven circulation
Dryers: Bulk airflow patterns, heat distribution (non-critical wall effects)
Early Design Studies: Concept evaluation, parametric studies, feasibility analysis
Large-Scale Systems: Factory ventilation, building HVAC integration studies

5. When to Choose k-ε Over SST

  • Time-critical projects: Fast turnaround required for design iterations and parametric studies.
  • High Reynolds flows: Re > 50,000, fully developed turbulence dominates.
  • Simple geometries: Straight ducts, channels, basic heat exchangers.
  • Engineering accuracy acceptable: ±15-20% error margins sufficient for design decisions.
  • Limited computational resources: Quick studies on standard workstation hardware.

6. When NOT to Use k-ε

Wall-bounded flows requiring accuracy: Heat transfer, friction, near-wall mixing
Separation-dominated flows: Airfoils, diffusers, sudden expansions
Swirling flows: Cyclones, tangential inlets, rotating machinery
Low Reynolds number flows: Re < 10,000, laminar-turbulent transition
Impingement flows: Jet impingement, complex wall interactions
Research applications: When ±5% accuracy is required

7. Setup and Mesh Guidelines

Mesh Requirements:

  • y⁺ > 30: Essential for wall function validity (optimal range: 30-300).
  • Growth ratio: < 1.3 acceptable, more forgiving than SST model.
  • Aspect ratio: < 100 in boundary layer regions sufficient.
  • Wall normal cells: 5-10 cells sufficient due to wall function approach.

Solver Settings:

  • Discretization: First-order upwind for stability, second-order for accuracy.
  • Convergence: Residuals < 10⁻⁴ typically sufficient for engineering accuracy.
  • Under-relaxation: k: 0.8, ε: 0.8 (more aggressive than SST).

Boundary Conditions:

  • Inlet turbulence: I = 1-10%, ℓ = 0.07 × hydraulic diameter for internal flows.
  • Wall conditions: Standard wall functions, no-slip velocity condition.
  • Outlet conditions: Zero gradient for k and ε typically adequate.

8. Performance Expectations

Accuracy Levels:

  • Pressure drop: ±10-15% for simple internal flows, ±20-30% for complex geometries.
  • Heat transfer: ±15-25% for attached flows, poor for separation zones.
  • Flow patterns: Reliable for attached flows, qualitative for separated regions.
  • Mixing prediction: Adequate for high-Re jets and far-field mixing.

Computational Performance:

  • vs SST: 40-50% faster solution time, 60-70% fewer mesh cells required.
  • Memory usage: 30-40% lower due to coarser mesh and simpler wall treatment.
  • Convergence rate: Typically converges in 50-70% fewer iterations than SST.

9. Common Pitfalls and Solutions

Mesh-Related Issues:

  • Problem: y⁺ < 30 causing wall function breakdown. Solution: Coarsen near-wall mesh or use enhanced wall treatment.
  • Problem: Insufficient resolution in shear layers. Solution: Refine mesh in mixing regions.

Physical Modeling Issues:

  • Problem: Poor separation prediction. Solution: Consider SST or RSM models for critical separation.
  • Problem: Jet spreading rate over-prediction. Solution: Use RNG or Realizable k-ε variants.

Boundary Condition Issues:

  • Problem: Inlet turbulence specification uncertainty. Solution: Use turbulence intensity I = 5% and length scale ℓ = 0.07D as default.
  • Problem: Backflow at outlets causing instability. Solution: Move outlet boundary downstream or specify turbulence values.

Historical Context and Development

The k-ε model represents a milestone in turbulence modeling, being the first complete closure for the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes equations. Developed by Launder and Spalding (1972-1974), it established the foundation for modern industrial CFD.

Timeline and Evolution:

  • 1925: Prandtl introduces mixing length concept
  • 1945: Kolmogorov proposes k-ε framework foundations
  • 1967: Harlow & Nakayama develop first k-ε implementation
  • 1971: Jones & Launder establish two-equation modeling principles
  • 1974: Launder & Spalding finalize standard k-ε model constants
  • 1992: Yakhot & Orszag introduce RNG k-ε variant using renormalization group theory
  • 1995: Shih et al. develop Realizable k-ε ensuring mathematical realizability

Mathematical Foundation and Complete Derivation

The k-ε model emerges from the exact Reynolds stress transport equations through systematic modeling approximations and the Boussinesq eddy viscosity hypothesis.

Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes Equations:

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho \overline{u_i})}{\partial x_i} = 0$$
$$\frac{\partial(\rho \overline{u_i})}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho \overline{u_i u_j})}{\partial x_j} = -\frac{\partial \overline{p}}{\partial x_i} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\mu\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right) - \rho\overline{u_i'u_j'}\right]$$

Exact Turbulent Kinetic Energy Equation (k = ½τᵢᵢ):

$$\frac{\partial(\rho k)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho k u_i)}{\partial x_i} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\mu + \frac{\mu_t}{\sigma_k}\right) \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right] + G_k + G_b - \rho\epsilon - Y_M + S_k$$

Modeled Dissipation Rate Transport Equation:

$$\frac{\partial(\rho \epsilon)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho \epsilon u_i)}{\partial x_i} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[\left(\mu + \frac{\mu_t}{\sigma_\epsilon}\right) \frac{\partial \epsilon}{\partial x_j}\right] + C_{1\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{k}(G_k + C_{3\epsilon}G_b) - C_{2\epsilon}\rho\frac{\epsilon^2}{k} + S_\epsilon$$

Boussinesq Eddy Viscosity Hypothesis:

$$-\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'} = \mu_t \left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right) - \frac{2}{3}\left(\rho k + \mu_t \frac{\partial \overline{u_k}}{\partial x_k}\right)\delta_{ij}$$

Turbulent Viscosity from Dimensional Analysis:

$$\mu_t = \rho C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\epsilon}, \quad \text{where } [\mu_t] = \frac{[k]^2}{[\epsilon]} = \frac{(L^2T^{-2})^2}{L^2T^{-3}} = ML^{-1}T^{-1}$$

Derivation of ε-equation from Scale Arguments:

The ε-equation structure follows from Kolmogorov's dimensional analysis:

$$\epsilon \sim \frac{k^{3/2}}{\ell}, \quad \text{where } \ell \text{ is the integral length scale}$$

This leads to the modeled equation structure ensuring dimensional consistency.

Comprehensive Term Definitions and Physical Meaning

k: Turbulent kinetic energy per unit mass [m²/s²] $$k = \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'} = \frac{1}{2}(\overline{u'^2} + \overline{v'^2} + \overline{w'^2})$$

Physical meaning: Represents the average kinetic energy contained in turbulent fluctuations. Controls turbulent intensity and mixing capability. In isotropic turbulence, relates directly to turbulent velocity scale.

ε: Turbulent dissipation rate [m²/s³] $$\epsilon = \nu \overline{\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}\frac{\partial u_i'}{\partial x_j}} = 2\nu \overline{s_{ij}'s_{ij}'}$$

Physical meaning: Rate at which turbulent kinetic energy is irreversibly converted to internal energy through viscous dissipation at the Kolmogorov microscale. Determines turbulent time scale τₜ = k/ε.

μₜ: Turbulent (eddy) viscosity [kg/m·s] $$\mu_t = \rho C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\epsilon} = \rho \nu_t$$

Physical meaning: Represents enhanced momentum transport due to turbulent mixing. Typically 10²-10⁵ times larger than molecular viscosity in high-Re flows. Links turbulent stresses to mean flow gradients.

Gₖ: Shear production of turbulent kinetic energy [kg/m³·s³] $$G_k = -\rho \overline{u_i'u_j'} \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i} = \mu_t S^2 = \mu_t \left(2\overline{S_{ij}S_{ij}}\right)$$

Physical meaning: Energy transfer from mean flow to turbulent fluctuations through Reynolds stress work. Always positive (energy source). Proportional to mean shear rate squared.

Gᵦ: Buoyant production/destruction of turbulent kinetic energy [kg/m³·s³] $$G_b = \beta g_i \frac{\mu_t}{Pr_t} \frac{\partial T}{\partial x_i}, \quad \beta = -\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial T}\right)_p$$

Physical meaning: Gb > 0 for unstable stratification (buoyancy enhances turbulence), Gb < 0 for stable stratification (buoyancy suppresses turbulence). Important in natural convection.

S: Magnitude of mean strain rate tensor [1/s] $$S = \sqrt{2S_{ij}S_{ij}}, \quad S_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right)$$

Physical meaning: Measure of mean flow deformation rate. In simple shear flow, S = |∂u/∂y|. Controls production rate through shear-stress work.

Yₘ: Dilatation dissipation in compressible flows [kg/m³·s³] $$Y_M = 2\rho \epsilon M_t^2, \quad M_t = \sqrt{\frac{k}{a^2}} \text{ (turbulent Mach number)}$$

Physical meaning: Additional dissipation due to compressibility effects. Becomes significant when Mt > 0.25. Accounts for dilatational dissipation in compressible turbulence.

C₃ε: Buoyancy parameter in ε-equation $$C_{3\epsilon} = \tanh\left|\frac{v}{u}\right|$$

Physical meaning: Accounts for flow directionality in stratified flows. C₃ε = 1 for vertical flows (full buoyancy effects), C₃ε = 0 for horizontal flows (no buoyancy effects in ε-equation).

Turbulent Scales and Energy Cascade Theory

Kolmogorov Microscales (Universal Dissipation Scales):

$$\eta = \left(\frac{\nu^3}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/4}, \quad \tau_\eta = \left(\frac{\nu}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/2}, \quad u_\eta = (\nu\epsilon)^{1/4}$$

Where η = Kolmogorov length scale, τη = Kolmogorov time scale, uη = Kolmogorov velocity scale. These represent the smallest scales where viscous dissipation occurs.

Integral Scales (Energy-Containing Scales):

$$\ell_t = C_\mu^{3/4}\frac{k^{3/2}}{\epsilon}, \quad \tau_t = \frac{k}{\epsilon}, \quad u_t = \sqrt{k}$$

These scales characterize the largest, energy-containing eddies that are geometry-dependent and contain most of the turbulent kinetic energy.

Taylor Microscale (Inertial Subrange):

$$\lambda = \sqrt{\frac{15\nu k}{\epsilon}}, \quad Re_\lambda = \frac{u_t \lambda}{\nu} = \sqrt{15Re_t}$$

Intermediate scale between integral and Kolmogorov scales, important for characterizing inertial subrange behavior.

Scale Separation and Reynolds Number Requirements:

$$Re_t = \frac{k^2}{\nu\epsilon}, \quad \frac{\ell_t}{\eta} \sim Re_t^{3/4}, \quad \frac{\tau_t}{\tau_\eta} \sim Re_t^{1/2}$$

For the k-ε model validity, clear scale separation requires Ret >> 1. Typically Ret > 200 is needed for reliable predictions.

Energy Cascade and Kolmogorov Hypothesis:

$$E(κ) = C_K \epsilon^{2/3} κ^{-5/3} \quad \text{(inertial subrange)}$$

Where E(κ) is energy spectrum, κ is wavenumber. The -5/3 law represents universal behavior in the inertial subrange.

Model Constants: Derivation and Physical Significance

$$C_\mu = 0.09, \quad C_{1\epsilon} = 1.44, \quad C_{2\epsilon} = 1.92$$ $$\sigma_k = 1.0, \quad \sigma_\epsilon = 1.3, \quad \kappa = 0.41 \text{ (von Kármán constant)}$$

Detailed Derivation of Model Constants:

Cμ = 0.09: Derived from logarithmic law requirements in equilibrium boundary layers $$C_\mu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{2\epsilon} - C_{1\epsilon}}} \cdot \frac{1}{\kappa^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.92-1.44}} \cdot \frac{1}{0.41^2} \approx 0.09$$

Ensures correct behavior in the logarithmic region of wall-bounded flows where Pk = ε.

C1ε = 1.44: Calibrated from homogeneous shear flows $$C_{1\epsilon} = 1 + \frac{2S^*}{\sqrt{C_\mu}}, \quad S^* \approx 0.28 \text{ (empirical)}$$

Ensures equilibrium Pk/ε ≈ 1.8 in homogeneous shear flows, matching experimental data.

C2ε = 1.92: Controls dissipation rate in decaying turbulence $$C_{2\epsilon} = C_{1\epsilon} + \frac{\kappa^2}{\sqrt{C_\mu}} = 1.44 + \frac{0.41^2}{\sqrt{0.09}} \approx 1.92$$

Ensures proper decay rate k ∝ t-n with n ≈ 1.3 in isotropic turbulence decay.

σk = 1.0, σε = 1.3: Turbulent Schmidt/Prandtl numbers $$\nu_t^k = \frac{\mu_t}{\rho \sigma_k}, \quad \nu_t^\epsilon = \frac{\mu_t}{\rho \sigma_\epsilon}$$

Based on analogy with heat transfer and calibration against experimental data for turbulent transport rates.

Historical Calibration Database:

  • Homogeneous shear flows: Pk/ε = 1.8 ± 0.1 (equilibrium ratio)
  • Free jets: Spreading rate β = 0.094 (exp: 0.086 ± 0.008)
  • Mixing layers: Growth rate δω/x = 0.18 (exp: 0.16 ± 0.02)
  • Boundary layers: Skin friction Cf predictions within ±5%
  • Isotropic decay: k/k₀ = (t/t₀)-1.3 (Comte-Bellot & Corrsin data)

Realizability Analysis:

$$\overline{u_i'u_j'}\overline{u_i'u_j'} \geq 0, \quad \overline{u_i'u_i'} \geq 0, \quad -\frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'} \leq \overline{u_i'u_j'} \leq \frac{1}{2}\overline{u_i'u_i'}$$

Standard k-ε model can violate these mathematical constraints in strong strain fields, leading to unphysical negative normal stresses.

Advanced Theoretical Framework and Fundamental Assumptions

Reynolds Decomposition and Statistical Framework:

$$u_i(x,t) = \overline{u_i}(x) + u_i'(x,t), \quad \overline{u_i'} = 0, \quad \overline{u_i'\overline{u_j}} = 0$$

For stationary turbulence, the ensemble average coincides with time average:

$$\overline{u_i} = \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \int_0^T u_i(x,t) dt$$

Exact Reynolds Stress Transport Equation:

$$\frac{D\overline{u_i'u_j'}}{Dt} = P_{ij} + \Phi_{ij} - \epsilon_{ij} + D_{ij}^T + D_{ij}^L + D_{ij}^M$$

Where:

  • Pij = Production by mean flow gradients
  • Φij = Pressure-strain redistribution
  • εij = Viscous dissipation tensor
  • Dij = Diffusion terms (turbulent, molecular, mean convection)

Boussinesq Approximation and Its Implications:

$$-\rho\overline{u_i'u_j'} = \mu_t \left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right) - \frac{2}{3}\left(\rho k + \mu_t \frac{\partial \overline{u_k}}{\partial x_k}\right)\delta_{ij}$$

This assumes:

  • Turbulent stresses align with mean strain rate (isotropic eddy viscosity)
  • Principal axes of stress and strain tensors coincide
  • Local equilibrium between production and dissipation

Anisotropy Tensor Analysis:

$$a_{ij} = \frac{\overline{u_i'u_j'}}{2k} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}, \quad \text{where } \sum_i a_{ii} = 0$$

For isotropic turbulence, aij = 0. The k-ε model assumes local isotropy, which fails in:

  • Strongly strained flows (∂u/∂y >> ∂v/∂x)
  • Flows with streamline curvature or rotation
  • Impingement regions with strong normal strain
  • Secondary flows in non-circular ducts

Critical Model Assumptions and Validity Limits:

  • High Reynolds number hypothesis: Ret = k²/(νε) >> 1, viscous effects negligible away from walls
  • Local equilibrium assumption: Pk ≈ ε in attached boundary layers (∂Pk/∂t ≈ ∂ε/∂t ≈ 0)
  • Gradient diffusion hypothesis: Turbulent transport ∝ gradient of transported quantity
  • Isotropic dissipation assumption: εij = (2/3)ε δij, small scales are isotropic
  • Kolmogorov time scale assumption: τt = k/ε represents characteristic eddy turnover time

Modeling of ε-equation and Its Justification:

The ε-equation is entirely modeled (not derived from first principles) based on:

$$\frac{D\epsilon}{Dt} \sim \frac{\epsilon}{\tau_t}P_k - \frac{\epsilon}{\tau_t}f(\text{dissipation}) + \text{diffusion}$$

Where τt = k/ε is the turbulent time scale. This structure ensures:

  • Dimensional consistency [ε] = L²T⁻³
  • Proper asymptotic behavior in simple flows
  • Correct scaling with turbulent time scale

Wall Treatment and Near-Wall Physics

Standard Wall Functions - Theoretical Foundation:

The k-ε model requires wall functions due to the ε → ∞ singularity at solid walls. The logarithmic law provides the bridge:

$$u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln(y^+) + B, \quad \text{where } u^+ = \frac{u}{u_\tau}, \quad y^+ = \frac{y u_\tau}{\nu}$$

With κ = 0.41 (von Kármán constant), B = 5.2 (smooth walls), and friction velocity:

$$u_\tau = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}}, \quad \text{where } \tau_w = \mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_{wall}$$

Near-Wall Asymptotic Behavior and Limitations:

The exact near-wall behavior for k and ε is:

$$k \sim y^2, \quad \epsilon \sim y^0 \text{ (constant)}, \quad \frac{k^2}{\epsilon} \sim y^4 \text{ as } y \to 0$$

This makes μt → 0 as y → 0, but the k-ε model cannot capture this without wall functions.

Wall Function Implementation:

  • y⁺ range: 30 < y⁺ < 300 for logarithmic law validity
  • Wall shear stress: τw = ρuτ² computed from log-law
  • ε at wall-adjacent cell: εP = uτ³/(κyP)
  • k boundary condition: ∂k/∂n = 0 at wall

Enhanced Wall Treatment (EWT):

For y⁺ < 30 regions, enhanced wall treatment blends:

$$u^+ = \begin{cases} y^+ & \text{if } y^+ < 11.225 \text{ (linear law)} \\ \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln(y^+) + B & \text{if } y^+ > 11.225 \text{ (log law)} \end{cases}$$

Wall Function Limitations and Failure Modes:

  • Adverse pressure gradients: Log-law breakdown, poor separation prediction
  • Heat/mass transfer: Thermal boundary layer not resolved, poor Nusselt number predictions
  • Low Reynolds number flows: y⁺ < 30 requirement violated
  • Rough walls: Modified B constant needed, geometry-dependent

Advanced Model Variants and Modern Improvements

RNG k-ε Model (Yakhot & Orszag, 1992):

Based on renormalization group theory, providing systematic derivation of model constants and additional strain-rate dependency:

$$C_\mu = 0.0845, \quad C_{1\epsilon} = 1.42, \quad C_{2\epsilon} = 1.68$$ $$\sigma_k = \sigma_\epsilon = 0.7179$$

Modified C2ε with strain-rate dependency:

$$C_{2\epsilon}^* = C_{2\epsilon} + \frac{C_\mu \eta^3 (1-\eta/\eta_0)}{1 + \beta\eta^3}, \quad \eta = S\frac{k}{\epsilon}, \quad \eta_0 = 4.38, \quad \beta = 0.012$$

This modification improves prediction of strained and rapidly distorted flows.

Realizable k-ε Model (Shih et al., 1995):

Ensures mathematical realizability conditions are satisfied, preventing unphysical negative normal stresses:

$$C_\mu = \frac{1}{A_0 + A_s \frac{kU^*}{\epsilon}}, \quad U^* = \sqrt{S_{ij}S_{ij} + \tilde{\Omega}_{ij}\tilde{\Omega}_{ij}}$$
$$A_0 = 4.04, \quad A_s = \sqrt{6}\cos\phi, \quad \phi = \frac{1}{3}\arccos(\sqrt{6}W)$$
$$W = \frac{S_{ij}S_{jk}S_{ki}}{S^3}, \quad \tilde{\Omega}_{ij} = \Omega_{ij} - 2\epsilon_{ijk}\omega_k$$

Low-Reynolds Number k-ε Models:

Modifications for integration to the wall, avoiding wall functions:

Launder-Sharma Model (1974):

$$C_\mu = C_\mu^* f_\mu, \quad f_\mu = \exp\left(-\frac{3.4}{(1+Re_t/50)^2}\right)$$ $$C_{2\epsilon} = C_{2\epsilon}^* f_2, \quad f_2 = 1 - 0.3\exp(-Re_t^2)$$

Abe-Kondoh-Nagano Model (1994):

$$f_\mu = \left(1 - \exp\left(-\frac{y^+}{14}\right)\right)^2 \left(1 + \frac{5}{Re_t^{3/4}}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{Re_t}{200}\right)^2\right)\right)$$

Rotation and Curvature Corrections:

Modifications for flows with significant rotation or streamline curvature:

$$C_\mu^* = C_\mu f_r, \quad f_r = \max\left[0, \frac{1-0.03\sqrt{\Omega k/\epsilon}}{1+0.23\sqrt{\Omega k/\epsilon}}\right]$$

Where Ω is the magnitude of mean rotation rate tensor:

$$\Omega = \sqrt{2\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{ij}}, \quad \Omega_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial \overline{u_i}}{\partial x_j} - \frac{\partial \overline{u_j}}{\partial x_i}\right)$$

Compressibility Corrections:

For high-speed flows with significant compressibility effects:

$$M_t = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{a^2}}, \quad Y_M = 2\rho \epsilon M_t^2$$
$$C_{2\epsilon}^* = C_{2\epsilon} + 0.2M_t^2 \text{ for } M_t > 0.25$$

Advanced Numerical Implementation and Stability

Source Term Linearization for Robustness:

Implicit treatment of destruction terms ensures numerical stability:

$$S_k = G_k - \rho\epsilon \rightarrow S_k = G_k - \rho\frac{\epsilon}{k}k = G_k - S_k^{impl} k$$
$$S_\epsilon = C_{1\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{k}G_k - C_{2\epsilon}\rho\frac{\epsilon^2}{k} \rightarrow C_{1\epsilon}\frac{\epsilon}{k}G_k - C_{2\epsilon}\rho\frac{\epsilon}{k}\epsilon$$

Realizability-Preserving Numerical Techniques:

  • Production limiter: Pk = min(Pk, 10ρε) prevents excessive production in stagnation regions
  • Lower bounds: kmin = 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s², εmin = 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s³ prevent division by zero
  • Time scale limiting: τt = max(k/ε, Cτ√(ν/ε)), Cτ ≈ 6 prevents unrealistic time scales
  • Viscosity ratio limiting: μt/μ < 10⁵ prevents excessive eddy viscosity

Advanced Discretization Schemes:

QUICK scheme for convection terms: $$\phi_f = \phi_P + \frac{r}{2}(\phi_E - \phi_P) - \frac{1-r}{8}(\phi_{EE} - 3\phi_E + 3\phi_P - \phi_W)$$

Third-order accuracy in smooth regions, reduces numerical diffusion compared to upwind schemes.

Central differencing with deferred correction: $$\nabla\phi|_f = \nabla\phi|_f^{CD} + \psi[\nabla\phi|_f^{HO} - \nabla\phi|_f^{CD}]$$

Where ψ is a blending factor (0 ≤ ψ ≤ 1) ensuring stability while maintaining accuracy.

Convergence Acceleration and Monitoring:

  • Multigrid methods: V-cycles and W-cycles for elliptic pressure correction equation
  • Algebraic multigrid (AMG): For unstructured grids and complex geometries
  • Block-coupled solvers: Solve momentum-k-ε equations simultaneously for better coupling
  • Adaptive under-relaxation: Automatic adjustment based on convergence behavior

Advanced Convergence Criteria and Monitoring:

$$\text{Scaled residuals: } R^n = \frac{|\sum_P a_P \phi_P^n - \sum_{nb} a_{nb} \phi_{nb}^n - S_P^n|}{\sum_P |a_P \phi_P^n|} < 10^{-4}$$
$$\text{Mass imbalance: } \frac{|\dot{m}_{in} - \dot{m}_{out}|}{|\dot{m}_{in}|} < 10^{-3}$$
$$\text{Integrated quantities: } \frac{|Q^n - Q^{n-100}|}{|Q^n|} < 10^{-3} \text{ (pressure drop, heat transfer, etc.)}$$

Mesh Quality Specifications for k-ε Model:

  • y⁺ requirements: 30 < y⁺ < 300 for standard wall functions
  • Aspect ratio: AR < 100 in boundary layer, AR < 1000 acceptable in core flow
  • Skewness: < 0.95 for tetrahedral cells, < 0.85 for high-quality solutions
  • Growth ratio: < 1.3 between adjacent cells, < 1.2 in high gradient regions
  • Minimum angle: > 10° for triangular faces, > 20° preferred

Solution Strategy and Best Practices:

  • Initialization: k = 1.5(UI)², ε = Cμ3/4k3/2/ℓ where I = turbulence intensity, ℓ = length scale
  • Solution sequence: First-order → second-order, coupled pressure-velocity, then turbulence equations
  • Monitoring: Track residuals, mass balance, and integrated engineering quantities

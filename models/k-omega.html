k-ω Standard Model

Two-equation RANS model solving transport equations for turbulent kinetic energy (k) and specific dissipation rate (ω)

🎯 CFD Guidance - k-ω Standard Model

✅ When to Use k-ω Standard (Critical Applications)

  • Near-wall flows - Superior low-Re treatment without wall functions
  • Adverse pressure gradient flows - Better separation prediction than k-ε
  • Transitional flows - Can handle some laminar-turbulent transition
  • Wall-bounded flows - Excellent boundary layer behavior
  • Internal flows - Pipes, ducts, channels with good wall treatment
  • Airfoil applications - Good performance for external aerodynamics
  • Low Reynolds number flows - Better than k-ε for Re < 10,000
  • Fine grid simulations - When y+ < 1 resolution is available

⚠️ When to Avoid k-ω Standard (Critical Limitations)

  • Free shear flows - Poor performance in jets, mixing layers, wakes
  • High Reynolds number external flows - Overpredicts turbulence in freestream
  • Flows with strong streamline curvature - Missing curvature corrections
  • Rotating or swirling flows - No rotation effects included
  • Rough wall applications - Standard model lacks roughness effects
  • Compressible flows - Needs compressibility corrections
  • Heat transfer applications - May need thermal modifications
  • Coarse grid simulations - Requires fine near-wall resolution

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Grid requirements: y+ < 1 at walls for accurate boundary layer resolution
  • Initialization: k = 1.5(UI)², ω = k^(1/2)/(0.3L) where I = turbulence intensity, L = length scale
  • Boundary conditions: ω = 6ν/(β₁Δy₁²) at walls, specify k and ω at inlets
  • Convergence: Monitor both k and ω residuals (< 1e-4) carefully
  • Realizability: Check for negative k or ω values, use limiters if needed
  • Freestream treatment: Use low ω values to avoid excessive freestream turbulence

📐 Transport Equations - Detailed Analysis

1️⃣ Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k) Equation

\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} =
P_k - \beta^* k \omega + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_k \nu_t)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Energy balance for turbulent kinetic energy

∂k/∂t + Uⱼ ∂k/∂xⱼ: Unsteady + convection terms

Pk: Production of k by mean shear = νₜ (∂Uᵢ/∂xⱼ)(∂Uᵢ/∂xⱼ + ∂Uⱼ/∂xᵢ)

-β* kω: Dissipation term, β* = 0.09

Diffusion: (ν + σₖνₜ) ∂²k/∂xⱼ² where σₖ = 0.5

2️⃣ Specific Dissipation Rate (ω) Equation

\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j} =
\alpha \frac{\omega}{k} P_k - \beta \omega^2 + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_\omega \nu_t)\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Transport equation for specific dissipation rate

∂ω/∂t + Uⱼ ∂ω/∂xⱼ: Unsteady + convection terms

α (ω/k)Pk: Production of ω proportional to k production, α = 5/9

-β ω²: Destruction of ω, β = 0.075

Diffusion: (ν + σωνₜ) ∂²ω/∂xⱼ² where σω = 0.5

Key difference from k-ε: ω has different units and behavior

3️⃣ Turbulent Viscosity Closure

\nu_t = \frac{k}{\omega}

Physical Meaning: Simple ratio of k to ω

Advantage over k-ε: More direct relationship, better near-wall behavior

ω definition: ω = ε/(β*k) where ε is dissipation rate

Dimensional check: [νₜ] = [k/ω] = (m²/s²)/(1/s) = m²/s ✓

Wall behavior: ω → ∞ as wall is approached, νₜ → 0

4️⃣ Relationship to k-ε Model

\omega = \frac{\varepsilon}{\beta^* k}, \quad \varepsilon = \beta^* k \omega

Physical Meaning: ω is specific dissipation rate (dissipation per unit k)

Units: [ω] = 1/s, [ε] = m²/s³

Conversion: Can transform between k-ε and k-ω formulations

β* = 0.09: Same as Cμ in k-ε model

Advantage: ω has better near-wall behavior than ε

💡 Frequency-Based Analogy

Think of k-ω as describing turbulence through energy (k) and frequency (ω). Like a sound system where k is the volume (energy) and ω is the frequency (how fast the energy cycles).

k equation: Tracks the "volume" of turbulent energy - how much energy is stored in the turbulent fluctuations.

ω equation: Controls the "frequency" of energy turnover - how fast the energy cycles through the turbulent cascade.

Near walls: ω becomes very large (high frequency) as the turbulent eddies get smaller and faster near the wall - like high-pitched sounds near a speaker.

Advantage over k-ε: ω naturally goes to the right value at walls without needing wall functions - like a speaker that automatically adjusts its frequency response.

⚖️ k-ω vs k-ε Comparison

✅ Advantages of k-ω over k-ε

  • Superior near-wall treatment - No wall functions required
  • Better low-Re performance - Natural damping near walls
  • Improved adverse pressure gradient handling - Better separation prediction
  • Transitional flow capability - Can handle some transition effects
  • Direct turbulent viscosity - νₜ = k/ω is simpler than k²/ε

❌ Disadvantages of k-ω vs k-ε

  • Poor free shear flow performance - Jets, mixing layers, wakes
  • Freestream sensitivity - Overpredicts turbulence away from walls
  • Higher grid requirements - Needs y+ < 1 for accuracy
  • Less robust for industrial flows - More specialized than k-ε
  • Boundary condition complexity - ω wall BC more complex than ε

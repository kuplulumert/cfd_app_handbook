Inviscid SST

SST-Inspired Pseudo-Turbulent Model for Enhanced Inviscid Flow Analysis

🎯
Model Overview

The Inviscid SST model applies the mathematical elegance and blending concepts of the k-ω SST turbulence model to inviscid flow analysis. Instead of turbulent quantities, it tracks pseudo-turbulent variables that capture flow acceleration, pressure gradients, and shock characteristics.

🧮 Complexity
Medium - More than Euler, less than RANS
⚡ Speed
Fast - Inviscid base with enhanced physics
🎯 Best For
High-speed flows, shock detection, design optimization
📊 Equations
5 PDEs (Euler + 2 pseudo-turbulent)

💡 Core Innovation

Combines inviscid efficiency with SST's adaptive blending philosophy to enhance flow feature detection and analysis without viscous computational overhead.

Mathematical Formulation
Base Euler Equations
$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}) = \mathbf{S}$$

Where $\mathbf{U} = [\rho, \rho \mathbf{u}, \rho E]^T$ and $\mathbf{S}$ includes pseudo-turbulent source terms

Pseudo-Turbulent Kinetic Energy (κ)
$$\frac{\partial \kappa}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \kappa = P_\kappa - \beta^* \kappa \varpi + \nabla \cdot [(\alpha_\kappa + \sigma_\kappa \nu_p) \nabla \kappa]$$

$\kappa$ represents flow acceleration intensity and pressure gradient magnitude

Pseudo-Dissipation Rate (ϖ)
$$\frac{\partial \varpi}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \varpi = \alpha \frac{\varpi}{\kappa} P_\kappa - \beta \varpi^2 + \nabla \cdot [(\alpha_\varpi + \sigma_\varpi \nu_p) \nabla \varpi] + 2(1-F_1)\frac{\rho}{\sigma_{\varpi 2} \varpi}\nabla \kappa \cdot \nabla \varpi$$

$\varpi$ characterizes the rate of flow feature dissipation - includes SST cross-diffusion term

Pseudo-Turbulent Production
$$P_\kappa = \nu_p \left|\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}\right|^2 + C_p \frac{|\nabla p|^2}{\rho^2 c^2} + C_s \left(\frac{|\nabla \rho|}{\rho}\right)^2$$

Production from velocity gradients, pressure gradients, and density gradients (shocks)

Blending Function (SST-Inspired)
$$F_1 = \tanh\left[\min\left(\max\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{\beta^* \varpi \delta}, \frac{500 c}{\delta^2 \varpi}\right), \frac{4\rho \kappa}{\sigma_{\varpi 2} D_{\varpi}^+ \delta^2}\right)^4\right]$$

Blends between shock-adapted (near discontinuities) and acceleration-adapted (smooth regions) formulations

Pseudo-Viscosity
$$\nu_p = \frac{a_1 \kappa}{\max(a_1 \varpi, |\mathbf{\Omega}| F_2)} \cdot \left(1 + C_{shock} \frac{|\nabla \rho|}{\rho}\right)$$

Pseudo-viscosity for enhanced shock capturing and flow feature resolution

Implementation Notes
🔢 Numerical Scheme
Coupled implicit, upwind for pseudo-turbulent transport
🎯 Initialization
κ = 1e-6, ϖ = 1e-3 (low background values)
🔧 Convergence
Monitor κ and ϖ residuals alongside standard Euler residuals
📊 Post-Processing
High κ regions indicate shocks/strong acceleration zones

💡 Practical Tips

  • Mesh requirements: Similar to inviscid Euler - no boundary layer refinement needed
  • Boundary conditions: Zero gradient for κ and ϖ at walls (slip condition maintained)
  • CFL considerations: Pseudo-turbulent transport may require slightly lower CFL than pure Euler
  • Validation approach: Compare Euler solution, then analyze κ/ϖ distributions for flow insights

Summary

Inviscid SST represents an innovative approach to inviscid flow analysis, applying the proven mathematical framework of k-ω SST to track pseudo-turbulent quantities that reveal flow physics beyond basic Euler equations.

This model maintains computational efficiency while providing enhanced insight into flow acceleration, pressure gradients, and shock characteristics - making it ideal for high-speed flow analysis and aerodynamic design optimization.

⚠️ Limitations

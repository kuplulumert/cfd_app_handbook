🎯 CFD Guidance - k-ω SST Model

✅ When to Use k-ω SST (Industry Standard)

  • General-purpose CFD - Best overall performance for most applications
  • External aerodynamics - Aircraft, automotive, wind engineering
  • Turbomachinery - Compressors, turbines, fans, pumps
  • Heat exchangers - Internal flows with heat transfer
  • Adverse pressure gradients - Superior separation prediction
  • Mixed flow types - Wall-bounded + free shear regions
  • Industrial CFD - Standard choice in commercial codes
  • Design optimization - Good balance of accuracy and cost

⚠️ When to Consider Alternatives

  • Pure free shear flows - k-ε might be better for jets, mixing layers
  • Transitional flows - Need transition SST or γ-Reθ models
  • Rotating flows - Need rotation/curvature corrections
  • Very low Re flows - Consider laminar or low-Re models
  • Rough walls - Need roughness modifications
  • Computational efficiency critical - k-ε might be sufficient
  • Research accuracy - Consider Reynolds Stress Model

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Grid requirements: y+ < 1 for best results, can work with y+ < 300 with wall functions
  • Initialization: Same as k-ω: k = 1.5(UI)², ω = k^(1/2)/(0.3L)
  • Boundary conditions: Standard k-ω BCs, ω wall treatment automatic
  • Convergence: Generally more robust than standard k-ω
  • Blending: F₁ function automatically switches between formulations
  • Recommended choice: Default for most industrial applications

📐 SST Equations - Detailed Analysis

1️⃣ Turbulent Kinetic Energy (k) Equation

\frac{\partial k}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j} =
P_k - \beta^* k \omega + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_k \nu_t)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Turbulent kinetic energy (same as standard k-ω)

∂k/∂t + Uⱼ ∂k/∂xⱼ: Unsteady + convection terms

Pk: Production by mean shear = νₜ (∂Uᵢ/∂xⱼ)(∂Uᵢ/∂xⱼ + ∂Uⱼ/∂xᵢ)

-β*kω: Dissipation term, β* = 0.09

Diffusion: (ν + σₖνₜ) ∂²k/∂xⱼ² where σₖ = F₁σₖ₁ + (1-F₁)σₖ₂

2️⃣ Specific Dissipation Rate (ω) Equation - SST Formulation

\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j} =
\alpha \frac{\omega}{k} P_k - \beta \omega^2 + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \sigma_\omega \nu_t)\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x_j}\right] + CD_{k\omega}

Physical Meaning: Hybrid ω equation with cross-diffusion term

∂ω/∂t + Uⱼ ∂ω/∂xⱼ: Transport of specific dissipation rate

α (ω/k)Pk: Production with blended coefficient α = F₁α₁ + (1-F₁)α₂

-β ω²: Destruction with blended β = F₁β₁ + (1-F₁)β₂

CDkω: Cross-diffusion term (key SST addition) = 2(1-F₁)σω₂(1/ω)(∂k/∂xⱼ)(∂ω/∂xⱼ)

Diffusion: (ν + σωνₜ) ∂²ω/∂xⱼ² with blended σω

3️⃣ Blending Function F₁

F_1 = \tanh\left(\min\left[\max\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{\beta^* \omega d}, \frac{500\nu}{\omega d^2}\right), \frac{4\sigma_{\omega 2} k}{CD_{k\omega} d^2}\right]^4\right)

Physical Meaning: Switches between k-ω (near walls) and k-ε (freestream)

F₁ = 1: k-ω formulation (near walls)

F₁ = 0: k-ε formulation (away from walls)

d: Distance to nearest wall

Automatic switching: No user intervention needed

4️⃣ Turbulent Viscosity with Strain Rate Limitation

\nu_t = \frac{a_1 k}{\max(a_1 \omega, \Omega F_2)}

Physical Meaning: Limited turbulent viscosity for adverse pressure gradients

a₁ = 0.31: Limiting coefficient

Ω: Magnitude of vorticity tensor

F₂: Second blending function

Key advantage: Prevents excessive νₜ in adverse pressure gradients

💡 Hybrid Model Analogy

Think of k-ω SST as a smart hybrid car that automatically switches between two engines - using the best engine for each driving condition without the driver needing to decide.

Near walls (k-ω mode): Like using the electric motor in city driving - precise, efficient, and perfect for stop-and-go conditions (boundary layers).

Away from walls (k-ε mode): Like switching to the gasoline engine on highways - robust, proven, and ideal for high-speed cruising (freestream flow).

Blending function F₁: Like the smart transmission that seamlessly switches between engines based on driving conditions - no manual intervention needed.

Cross-diffusion term: Like the hybrid system's energy recovery - captures energy that would be lost in the transition between modes.

Strain rate limiter: Like the car's stability control - prevents the system from going unstable in challenging conditions (adverse pressure gradients).

🚀 SST Key Features

Hybrid Formulation

Combines the best of both worlds: k-ω accuracy near walls with k-ε robustness in freestream. Automatic blending via F₁ function eliminates user guesswork.

Cross-Diffusion Innovation

The CDkω term captures the mathematical transformation between k-ε and k-ω formulations, ensuring consistent physics across the blending region.

Strain Rate Limitation

Prevents excessive turbulent viscosity in adverse pressure gradients by limiting νₜ based on strain rate magnitude. Critical for separation prediction.

⚖️ SST vs Standard Models

✅ Advantages over k-ω and k-ε

  • Best of both worlds: k-ω accuracy + k-ε robustness
  • Superior separation prediction: Better than both parent models
  • Automatic model switching: No user intervention required
  • Industry validation: Extensively validated in commercial applications
  • Robust convergence: More stable than standard k-ω
  • Strain rate limitation: Handles adverse pressure gradients
  • Wide applicability: Works for most flow types

❌ Limitations

  • Higher complexity: More complex than individual parent models
  • Computational cost: Slightly higher than k-ε due to blending
  • Still RANS limitations: Cannot predict transition without add-ons
  • Wall distance dependency: Requires wall distance calculation
  • Free shear flow limitations: Still some issues inherited from k-ω

Fundamental flow model based on Euler equations assuming perfect fluid with zero viscosity

🎯 CFD Guidance - Inviscid Flow

✅ When to Use Inviscid Flow (Critical Applications)

  • High Reynolds number external flows (Re > 10⁶) - Viscous forces negligible compared to inertial forces
  • Far-field flow analysis around objects - Away from boundary layers where viscosity dominates
  • Supersonic and hypersonic flows - Shock waves and compressibility effects dominate over viscosity
  • Initial design studies - Quick conceptual analysis before detailed viscous calculations
  • Potential flow regions - Core flow areas where viscous effects are minimal
  • Computational efficiency critical - When speed is more important than viscous accuracy
  • Inviscid flow benchmarking - Validating CFD codes against analytical solutions

⚠️ When to Avoid Inviscid Flow (Critical Limitations)

  • ANY flow with significant viscous effects - Boundary layers, wake regions, viscous sublayers
  • Boundary layer analysis - No-slip conditions cannot be satisfied (infinite velocity gradients)
  • Internal flows (pipes, ducts) - Wall friction dominates, viscous losses critical
  • Low Reynolds number flows (Re < 10⁴) - Viscous forces comparable to inertial forces
  • Heat transfer calculations - No thermal boundary layers, infinite heat transfer rates
  • Flows with separation/recirculation - Viscous effects cause flow detachment
  • Drag force predictions - Cannot predict viscous (friction) drag components

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Slip boundary conditions at walls - Tangential velocity ≠ 0, only normal velocity = 0
  • Focus on pressure and velocity distributions - Primary flow variables in inviscid analysis
  • Monitor mass conservation - ∇·(ρu) = 0 must be satisfied everywhere
  • Check for shock formation in compressible flow - Discontinuities in flow properties
  • Validate against potential flow theory - For incompressible, irrotational cases

📐 Euler Equations - Detailed Analysis

1️⃣ Continuity Equation (Mass Conservation)

\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0

Physical Meaning: Mass cannot be created or destroyed in the flow

∂ρ/∂t: Rate of density change at a fixed point in space

∇·(ρu): Net mass flux divergence = ∂(ρu)/∂x + ∂(ρv)/∂y + ∂(ρw)/∂z

= 0: Mass accumulation rate + Net mass outflow rate = 0

Expanded Form: ∂ρ/∂t + ∂(ρu)/∂x + ∂(ρv)/∂y + ∂(ρw)/∂z = 0

2️⃣ Momentum Equation (Newton's 2nd Law)

\frac{\partial (\rho \mathbf{u})}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u}) + \nabla p = \rho \mathbf{f}

Physical Meaning: Force = Mass × Acceleration (F = ma) for fluid elements

∂(ρu)/∂t: Rate of momentum change at a fixed point (local acceleration)

∇·(ρu⊗u): Convective momentum transport = ρu·∇u (advective acceleration)

∇p: Pressure gradient force = ∂p/∂x î + ∂p/∂y ĵ + ∂p/∂z k̂

ρf: Body forces per unit volume (gravity, electromagnetic, etc.)

Missing Terms: Viscous stresses (τ) = 0 in inviscid flow assumption

Vector Form: Each component (x,y,z) gives separate scalar equations

3️⃣ Energy Equation (1st Law of Thermodynamics)

\frac{\partial E}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot ((E + p) \mathbf{u}) = \rho \mathbf{f} \cdot \mathbf{u}

Physical Meaning: Energy conservation including kinetic + internal energy

E: Total energy per unit volume = ρ(e + u²/2) = ρe + ρu²/2

e: Specific internal energy (thermal energy per unit mass)

u²/2: Specific kinetic energy = (u² + v² + w²)/2

∂E/∂t: Rate of total energy change at fixed point

∇·((E+p)u): Net energy flux = convection of total energy + pressure work

ρf·u: Power input from body forces = force × velocity

Missing Terms: Heat conduction (q) and viscous dissipation (Φ) = 0

🔢 Mathematical Properties

System Type: Hyperbolic PDE system (wave-like behavior)

Variables: 5 unknowns (ρ, u, v, w, p) in 3D

Equations: 5 equations (1 continuity + 3 momentum + 1 energy)

Closure: Equation of state p = p(ρ,T) needed for closure

Characteristics: Information propagates at acoustic speed c = √(∂p/∂ρ)

Eigenvalues: u±c, u, u, u (5 characteristic speeds)

Conservation Form: ∂U/∂t + ∇·F(U) = S where U = [ρ, ρu, ρv, ρw, E]ᵀ

💡 Perfect Fluid Analogy

Think of inviscid flow as a perfect, frictionless fluid - like an idealized water flowing through a pipe with absolutely no friction. There's no viscosity, no boundary layers, and no energy loss due to internal friction.

Mass conservation: What flows in must flow out - like water through a pipe system.

Momentum conservation: Forces only come from pressure gradients and external forces - no viscous stress.

Energy conservation: Total energy is conserved in the absence of viscous dissipation.

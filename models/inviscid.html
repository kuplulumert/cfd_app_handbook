Inviscid Flow Model

Fundamental flow model based on Euler equations assuming perfect fluid with zero viscosity

📐 Euler Equations

\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u}) = 0
\frac{\partial (\rho \mathbf{u})}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{u} \otimes \mathbf{u}) + \nabla p = \rho \mathbf{f}
\frac{\partial E}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot ((E + p) \mathbf{u}) = \rho \mathbf{f} \cdot \mathbf{u}

🔤 Variables

ρ
Density
Fluid density (kg/m³)
u
Velocity Vector
Fluid velocity field (m/s)
p
Pressure
Static pressure (Pa)
E
Total Energy
E = ρ(e + u²/2)

💡 Perfect Fluid Analogy

Think of inviscid flow as a perfect, frictionless fluid - like an idealized water flowing through a pipe with absolutely no friction. There's no viscosity, no boundary layers, and no energy loss due to internal friction.

Mass conservation: What flows in must flow out - like water through a pipe system.

Momentum conservation: Forces only come from pressure gradients and external forces - no viscous stress.

Energy conservation: Total energy is conserved in the absence of viscous dissipation.

🎯 CFD Guidance

When to Use

  • High Reynolds number external flows (Re > 10⁶)
  • Far-field flow analysis around objects
  • Supersonic and hypersonic flow analysis
  • Initial design studies and conceptual analysis
  • When viscous effects are negligible
  • Computational efficiency is critical

When to Avoid

  • Any flow with significant viscous effects
  • Boundary layer analysis
  • Internal flows (pipes, ducts)
  • Low Reynolds number flows (Re < 10⁴)
  • Heat transfer calculations
  • Flows with separation or recirculation

Setup Tips

  • Use slip boundary conditions at walls
  • Focus on pressure and velocity distributions
  • Monitor mass conservation carefully
  • Check for shock wave formation in compressible flow
  • Validate against potential flow theory when possible

📋 Key Assumptions

Assumption Implication Validity
Zero Viscosity No viscous stresses High Re flows only
No Heat Conduction No thermal diffusion Adiabatic processes
Slip Walls No boundary layers External flow regions
Inviscid Stress Only pressure forces Away from solid boundaries

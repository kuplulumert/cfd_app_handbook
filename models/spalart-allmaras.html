S-A

Spalart-Allmaras

One-equation RANS model - Aerospace legacy model for external aerodynamics

✅
Use Spalart-Allmaras When (Industry Focus)
Situation Why It Works Advice
External aerodynamics (aircraft, missiles) Designed specifically for aerospace applications Legacy aerospace standard, well-validated
Boundary layer flows (airfoils, wings) Excellent near-wall behavior, good separation Good for attached and mildly separated flows
Simple CFD codes (one equation advantage) Lower memory, simpler implementation When computational simplicity matters
Legacy aerospace codes (established workflows) Extensively validated in aerospace industry Maintain compatibility with existing processes
Wall-bounded flows (channels, ducts) Good wall treatment, reasonable accuracy Alternative to k-ω for wall-bounded flows
⚠️
Avoid Spalart-Allmaras When (Industry Focus)
Situation Why It Fails Industry Advice
Modern aerospace projects (new designs) k-ω SST generally more accurate and versatile Use k-ω SST for new aerospace projects
Industrial flows (HVAC, pumps, process equipment) Not optimized for industrial applications Use k-ε for industrial equipment
Free shear flows (jets, mixing layers) Poor performance in free turbulence Use k-ε or k-ω for free shear
Complex 3D flows (automotive, turbomachinery) Limited physics compared to two-equation models Use k-ω SST for complex 3D flows
Heat transfer applications Lacks thermal modeling capabilities Use k-ε or k-ω for thermal analysis
Rotating flows (turbines, mixers) No rotation corrections built-in Use RSM or rotation-corrected models

Industry Takeaway

✅ Aerospace Legacy

Use for legacy aerospace codes and established workflows - proven track record.

⚡ One Equation Simple

Simpler than two-equation models but less versatile - k-ω SST usually better choice.

🎯 Specific Domain

Best for external aerodynamics - not recommended for industrial or automotive applications.

Transport Equation
Modified Turbulent Viscosity (ν̃)
$$\frac{\partial \tilde{\nu}}{\partial t} + u_j \frac{\partial \tilde{\nu}}{\partial x_j} = c_{b1} \tilde{S} \tilde{\nu}$$
$$- \left[c_{w1} f_w - \frac{c_{b1}}{\kappa^2} f_{t2}\right] \left(\frac{\tilde{\nu}}{d}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \tilde{\nu}) \frac{\partial \tilde{\nu}}{\partial x_j}\right] + \frac{c_{b2}}{\sigma}\left(\frac{\partial \tilde{\nu}}{\partial x_j}\right)^2$$

Single transport equation for modified turbulent viscosity

Turbulent Viscosity
$$\nu_t = \tilde{\nu} f_{v1}, \quad f_{v1} = \frac{\chi^3}{\chi^3 + c_{v1}^3}, \quad \chi = \frac{\tilde{\nu}}{\nu}$$

Damping function ensures proper near-wall behavior

Engineering Analogy

🎯 Specialized Tool

Spalart-Allmaras is like a specialized aerospace tool:

  • Single purpose: "Designed specifically for aircraft and missiles"
  • Simple design: "One equation instead of two - easier to implement"
  • Proven track record: "Trusted by aerospace industry for decades"
  • Limited scope: "Works great for its intended purpose, not general-purpose"
  • Being replaced: "Newer tools (k-ω SST) are more versatile and accurate"

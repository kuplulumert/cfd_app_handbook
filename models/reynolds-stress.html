Reynolds Stress Model

Seven-equation RANS model solving transport equations for all six Reynolds stress components plus dissipation rate

🛠️ Practical Applications

🎯 Excellent For:

  • • Complex 3D flows with anisotropy
  • • Rotating machinery (turbines, pumps)
  • • Swirling flows and cyclones
  • • Flows with strong streamline curvature
  • • Secondary flow predictions
  • • Research and validation studies

⚠️ Avoid For:

  • • Simple 2D flows
  • • Preliminary design studies
  • • Large-scale simulations
  • • Limited computational resources
  • • Quick engineering estimates
  • • Parametric studies

🎯 CFD Guidance - Reynolds Stress Model

✅ When to Use Reynolds Stress Model (Critical Applications)

  • Complex 3D flows - When turbulence anisotropy is critical
  • Rotating machinery - Turbines, compressors, pumps with rotation effects
  • Swirling flows - Cyclones, combustors, inlet distortions
  • Strong streamline curvature - Curved ducts, bends, diffusers
  • Secondary flow predictions - Corner flows, duct flows
  • Research applications - When highest accuracy is needed
  • Validation studies - Benchmark cases for model development
  • Anisotropic turbulence - When eddy viscosity assumption fails

⚠️ When to Avoid Reynolds Stress Model (Critical Limitations)

  • Simple 2D flows - Overkill for flows where simpler models work
  • Preliminary design studies - Too expensive for initial analysis
  • Large-scale simulations - 7 equations = high computational cost
  • Limited computational resources - Memory and CPU intensive
  • Industrial routine analysis - k-ε or k-ω often sufficient
  • Convergence challenges - More difficult to converge than simpler models
  • Parametric studies - Too slow for design optimization
  • Unsteady flows - Very expensive for time-dependent simulations

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Grid requirements: Very fine grids needed, y+ < 1 essential
  • Initialization: Initialize all 6 stress components carefully
  • Convergence: Monitor all 7 equation residuals, use relaxation
  • Realizability: Use realizability constraints to avoid non-physical states
  • Wall treatment: Requires sophisticated near-wall modeling
  • Computational cost: 3-5x more expensive than k-ε

📐 Reynolds Stress Transport Equations

1️⃣ Reynolds Stress Transport (6 equations)

\frac{\partial \overline{u_i' u_j'}}{\partial t} + U_k \frac{\partial \overline{u_i' u_j'}}{\partial x_k} =
P_{ij} + \Pi_{ij} - \varepsilon_{ij} + D_{ij}

Physical Meaning: Transport of each Reynolds stress component

Pᵢⱼ: Production by mean strain

Πᵢⱼ: Pressure-strain redistribution (modeled)

εᵢⱼ: Dissipation tensor (modeled)

Dᵢⱼ: Diffusion (modeled)

2️⃣ Dissipation Rate (ε) Equation

\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j} =
C_{1\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon}{k} P_k - C_{2\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon^2}{k} + \text{diffusion}

Physical Meaning: Similar to k-ε dissipation equation

Used for: Length scale information

C₁ε = 1.44, C₂ε = 1.92: Standard constants

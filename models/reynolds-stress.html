Reynolds Stress Model

Seven-equation RANS model solving transport equations for all six Reynolds stress components plus dissipation rate

🛠️ Practical Applications

🎯 Excellent For:

  • • Complex 3D flows with anisotropy
  • • Rotating machinery (turbines, pumps)
  • • Swirling flows and cyclones
  • • Flows with strong streamline curvature
  • • Secondary flow predictions
  • • Research and validation studies

⚠️ Avoid For:

  • • Simple 2D flows
  • • Preliminary design studies
  • • Large-scale simulations
  • • Limited computational resources
  • • Quick engineering estimates
  • • Parametric studies

🎯 CFD Guidance - Reynolds Stress Model

✅ When to Use Reynolds Stress Model (Critical Applications)

  • Complex 3D flows - When turbulence anisotropy is critical
  • Rotating machinery - Turbines, compressors, pumps with rotation effects
  • Swirling flows - Cyclones, combustors, inlet distortions
  • Strong streamline curvature - Curved ducts, bends, diffusers
  • Secondary flow predictions - Corner flows, duct flows
  • Research applications - When highest accuracy is needed
  • Validation studies - Benchmark cases for model development
  • Anisotropic turbulence - When eddy viscosity assumption fails

⚠️ When to Avoid Reynolds Stress Model (Critical Limitations)

  • Simple 2D flows - Overkill for flows where simpler models work
  • Preliminary design studies - Too expensive for initial analysis
  • Large-scale simulations - 7 equations = high computational cost
  • Limited computational resources - Memory and CPU intensive
  • Industrial routine analysis - k-ε or k-ω often sufficient
  • Convergence challenges - More difficult to converge than simpler models
  • Parametric studies - Too slow for design optimization
  • Unsteady flows - Very expensive for time-dependent simulations

Setup Tips & Best Practices

  • Grid requirements: Very fine grids needed, y+ < 1 essential
  • Initialization: Initialize all 6 stress components carefully
  • Convergence: Monitor all 7 equation residuals, use relaxation
  • Realizability: Use realizability constraints to avoid non-physical states
  • Wall treatment: Requires sophisticated near-wall modeling
  • Computational cost: 3-5x more expensive than k-ε

📐 Reynolds Stress Transport Equations - Detailed Analysis

1️⃣ Reynolds Stress Transport (6 equations)

\frac{\partial \overline{u_i' u_j'}}{\partial t} + U_k \frac{\partial \overline{u_i' u_j'}}{\partial x_k} =
P_{ij} + \Pi_{ij} - \varepsilon_{ij} + D_{ij}

Physical Meaning: Exact transport equation for each Reynolds stress component

∂u'ᵢu'ⱼ/∂t + Uₖ ∂u'ᵢu'ⱼ/∂xₖ: Unsteady + convection (exact terms)

Pᵢⱼ = -u'ᵢu'ₖ ∂Uⱼ/∂xₖ - u'ⱼu'ₖ ∂Uᵢ/∂xₖ: Exact production by mean strain

Πᵢⱼ: Pressure-strain redistribution (requires modeling)

εᵢⱼ: Dissipation tensor (requires modeling)

Dᵢⱼ: Turbulent + molecular diffusion (requires modeling)

2️⃣ Pressure-Strain Redistribution Model

\Pi_{ij} = \Pi_{ij,1} + \Pi_{ij,2} + \Pi_{ij,w}
\Pi_{ij,1} = -C_1 \varepsilon \left(a_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\right)

Physical Meaning: Redistributes energy between stress components

Πᵢⱼ,₁: Linear return-to-isotropy (slow term)

Πᵢⱼ,₂: Rapid pressure-strain (fast term)

Πᵢⱼ,w: Wall reflection effects

aᵢⱼ = u'ᵢu'ⱼ/k - (2/3)δᵢⱼ: Anisotropy tensor

C₁ ≈ 1.8: Return-to-isotropy coefficient

3️⃣ Dissipation Rate (ε) Equation

\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial t} + U_j \frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j} =
C_{1\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon}{k} P_k - C_{2\varepsilon} \frac{\varepsilon^2}{k} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[(\nu + \frac{\nu_t}{\sigma_\varepsilon})\frac{\partial \varepsilon}{\partial x_j}\right]

Physical Meaning: Length scale equation (same as k-ε model)

∂ε/∂t + Uⱼ ∂ε/∂xⱼ: Transport of dissipation rate

C₁ε (ε/k)Pk: Production proportional to turbulence production

-C₂ε ε²/k: Destruction of dissipation rate

Pk = ½Pᵢᵢ: Trace of production tensor

Purpose: Provides length scale for Reynolds stress transport

4️⃣ Turbulent Viscosity (For Momentum Equations)

\nu_t = C_\mu \frac{k^2}{\varepsilon}

Physical Meaning: Used only for mean momentum equations

Note: Reynolds stresses are computed directly, not via eddy viscosity

Cμ = 0.09: Standard coefficient

Usage: For computing mean velocity field from momentum equations

💡 Anisotropic Turbulence Analogy

Think of Reynolds Stress Model as tracking the complete "personality" of turbulence - not just its overall energy (like k-ε), but how it behaves differently in each direction.

Six stress components: Like measuring a person's mood in six different aspects - happiness, sadness, anxiety, excitement, calmness, and energy. Each can be different at the same time.

Anisotropy: Like recognizing that someone can be happy in one situation but anxious in another - turbulence can be strong in one direction but weak in another.

Pressure-strain redistribution: Like social dynamics where strong emotions in one area gradually spread to other areas - energy gets redistributed between different stress components.

Eddy viscosity assumption failure: Like assuming everyone has the same emotional response - clearly wrong for complex personalities (complex flows).

Seven equations advantage: Like having a detailed psychological profile instead of just asking "How do you feel?" - much more accurate but much more complex.

🧩 RSM Model Components

Reynolds Stress Tensor Structure

\tau_{ij} = \begin{bmatrix} u'_1 u'_1 & u'_1 u'_2 & u'_1 u'_3 \\ u'_2 u'_1 & u'_2 u'_2 & u'_2 u'_3 \\ u'_3 u'_1 & u'_3 u'_2 & u'_3 u'_3 \end{bmatrix}

Symmetric tensor: 6 independent components (3 normal + 3 shear stresses)

Exact vs Modeled Terms

Exact: Convection, Production. Modeled: Pressure-strain (Πᵢⱼ), Dissipation (εᵢⱼ), Diffusion (Dᵢⱼ). The modeling of these terms is where different RSM variants differ.

Computational Considerations

7 transport equations vs 2 for k-ε means ~3.5x more computational cost. Memory requirements also increase significantly. Convergence is more challenging due to coupling between equations.

🚀 RSM Key Features

No Eddy Viscosity Assumption

Directly computes Reynolds stresses without assuming isotropic turbulent viscosity. Captures anisotropic turbulence effects that k-ε and k-ω miss.

Exact Production Terms

Production terms Pᵢⱼ are exact (no modeling required). This gives superior accuracy in flows with strong mean strain, rotation, or curvature effects.

Complex Flow Capability

Handles rotating flows, swirling flows, flows with strong curvature, and secondary flows - all cases where simpler models fail due to anisotropy.

⚖️ RSM vs Eddy Viscosity Models

✅ Advantages over k-ε, k-ω, SST

  • No eddy viscosity assumption: Captures anisotropic turbulence
  • Exact production terms: Superior accuracy in complex strain fields
  • Rotation and curvature effects: Naturally included
  • Secondary flow prediction: Captures cross-stream flows
  • Swirling flow capability: Handles cyclones, combustors
  • Research accuracy: Highest fidelity RANS approach
  • Complex 3D flows: Superior to simpler models

❌ Disadvantages vs Simpler Models

  • Very high computational cost: 3-5x more expensive than k-ε
  • Memory requirements: Significantly higher storage needs
  • Convergence challenges: More difficult to achieve convergence
  • Implementation complexity: More complex coding and debugging
  • Modeling uncertainties: Pressure-strain and dissipation still modeled
  • Overkill for simple flows: Unnecessary for 2D attached flows
  • Industrial reluctance: Often avoided due to cost and complexity
